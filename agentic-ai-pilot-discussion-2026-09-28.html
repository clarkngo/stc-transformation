3. What would be a good first pilot?

Two candidates, run through Jim's 6-question framework

Pilot A (recommended): "Research-to-Brief" agent, starting with Space Economy / Project Leo

Problem
Tracking a fast-moving sector (launch costs, LEO commercial stations, workforce demand, competing programs) by hand takes hours and the notes are scattered.
Architecture
A deep-research step, then a synthesis agent with a fixed brief template, then Jim reviews. Runs as a saved Project plus a reusable prompt/skill, optionally on a weekly schedule. Output goes to a shared doc or knowledge base.
Info / systems
Public web sources, industry reports, and Jim's own notes. No institutional systems.
Automate vs. judge
Automate collecting, summarizing, citing, and comparing. Jim judges strategic implications, program positioning, and anything that goes to leadership.
Measure
Hours per brief, before vs. after. Jim's 1–5 rating on quality and usefulness. Share of claims with a verifiable source. Number of briefs Jim produces without me.
What Jim learns
Writing research prompts, structuring briefs, checking citations, spotting hallucinations, and then changing the workflow himself.

Pilot B: MBA 551 curriculum-refresh copilot

Problem
A refresh means benchmarking peer programs, aligning outcomes, and drafting new cases and assessments. The work is repetitive and heavy on synthesis.
Architecture
A Project loaded with the course outcomes and the current syllabus, plus agents for three tasks: peer benchmarking, an outcome-to-assessment map, and case/assignment drafts.
Info / systems
The public course description, Jim's own syllabus and materials, and public peer-program info. Check whether CityU course materials may go into the chosen tool.
Automate vs. judge
Automate the benchmarking tables and first drafts. Jim decides pedagogy, outcomes alignment, and the final content.
Measure
Time to a refresh-ready draft. Outcome-coverage gaps found. Jim's rating of the draft quality.
What Jim learns
Context engineering: what to feed the model, how templates constrain its output, and how to iterate.
Why Pilot A first: it uses public data only, so nobody else has to approve it. The result is visible within about two weeks, and the same research-to-brief pattern then carries over to MBA 551, the BAM pathway, and Habitat housing intel.

4. Which tools and capabilities should Jim master?

A learning ladder: each rung builds on the last
  1. Projects and context: Claude Projects or ChatGPT Projects with custom instructions and uploaded reference files. This is the foundation for everything else.
  2. Deep research modes: multi-step web research with citations, and how to check those citations critically.
  3. Source-grounded synthesis: NotebookLM, or Projects restricted to the uploaded sources, for board and strategic-plan documents.
  4. Templates and reusable skills: turning a good prompt into a repeatable workflow such as a brief template or a benchmark rubric.
  5. Connectors (MCP) and scheduling: letting the assistant read Drive and email, and running recurring briefs on a schedule.
  6. Desktop agents: Claude Cowork and similar agents for multi-step file and document work, with a human approving each consequential step.

Concepts that matter more than any one tool: context engineering, human-in-the-loop design, verification and evals, and data classification (what may go into which tool).
