How the AI 410 Marketing Video Was Made

A work-process note that adds to How AI 410 Was Built. It shows how a course lead and an AI assistant turned the finished student site into a marketing video, with no camera and no editing suite. It is written for program managers and colleagues who may want to do the same for another course.

1. The short version

Four things to take away if you read nothing else.

  1. A course built on the web already holds its own footage. The simulators, guides and example project on the public student site are the video. Nobody appears on camera, so there are no release forms.
  2. Script the recording, and the video stays maintainable. Every click and scroll is in a file. When the site changes, the video can be recorded again instead of edited by hand.
  3. Decisions stay with people, and production moves to the assistant. The course lead made the calls on audience, wording, voice and what to leave out. The assistant did the recording, editing and audio.
  4. An AI assistant can't review what it can't perceive. The assistant can't hear the voiceover. Checks caught a real pronunciation error, but a person still has to listen before anyone outside the team sees it.

5. How the plan changed

Each change took a re-run, not a re-shoot, because the plan lived in scripts and data files.

  1. PlanCaptions and light music

    The first plan had no spoken audio. The video had to work with the sound off.

  2. Plan"Upcoming" removed from the closing card

    The video no longer calls the course upcoming. It also never says students are already taking it. It speaks to the viewer as "you" and doesn't present a made-up student as real.

  3. BuildAn AI voiceover added

    A voice carries more explanation than captions can. The first version used the voice built into macOS, with no new install.

  4. BuildSwitched to Kokoro

    The course lead asked for the Kokoro neural voice. The script, scenes and captions stayed the same, and only the speech step changed.

  5. ShipThe video went into the repository

    The plan was to keep the file on YouTube only. It now plays on the plan page, so the team can review it there before any upload.

  6. ReviewMusic became a side-by-side choice

    Music was first going to be added at upload. Now reviewers compare a music version with the no-music one, and the no-music version stays as the master.

6. What went wrong

None of these was serious, and each was found by checking the output rather than trusting the script.

7. Lessons learned

What worked, what we would change, and a recommendation for each.

WorkedPlan on a page before building

The plan page came first, and it held the decisions, storyboard and rules. Changing a sentence there cost nothing, and it gave the course lead one place to say "remove this".

Recommendation: write the storyboard and rules down, and get a yes on them, before recording anything.

WorkedFootage from the real product

Because the student site is public, the video shows what students will actually use. It needed no mock-ups, sign-ins or student data.

Recommendation: record only public pages, and keep the LMS and anything with names or grades out of shot.

WorkedWords kept apart from the video

The narration lives in one data file, separate from the recording. Changing the voice or a line means one re-run, and the captions update with it, so they can't drift from what is said.

Recommendation: keep a single script file and generate the audio and captions from it. This is the "one source of truth" lesson from the course build, applied to media.

ChangeA check is not the same as listening

The assistant can't hear audio. Reading the planned sounds of each line caught "uh eye four ten", which an ordinary success message would have missed. That check can't judge whether the voice sounds natural, so human review stays on the sign-off list.

Recommendation: say plainly what automated checks can't cover, and name the person who covers it.

ChangeDecide the audio first

The audio plan changed twice, from captions only, to a built-in voice, to Kokoro. Each change was cheap, but the Kokoro install took the most troubleshooting of the whole job.

Recommendation: choose the voice at the planning stage, and test the tool on a single line before building around it.

ChangeCheck the wording rules for marketing early

Course pages and institutional copy follow different wording rules about a program that hasn't launched. The closing card needed a specific decision, because a video reaches people who see none of the surrounding context.

Recommendation: agree the claims a video may make before writing the script, and have marketing review the wording.

Change"Free music" still has conditions

A free license doesn't mean no copyright. The chosen track can be used without credit, but it can't be shared on its own, so the file stays out of the public repository. It is also registered with Content ID, which the track's page shows, so YouTube will probably flag it automatically even though the use is licensed.

Recommendation: read the license and the track's page before choosing. Save the license certificate with the download, and agree with marketing whether a claim on the channel is acceptable.

8. Playbook for the next course

The order to follow for an AI 420, 430 or 440 video once its student site is public. The scripts can be reused, with new scenes and narration.

  • PlanWrite the plan page: audience, length, what to leave out, and the claims the video may make. Get sign-off on the storyboard.
  • ScriptWrite the narration in narration.json. Each scene's lines should fit its length, with time to spare.
  • RecordAdd the new scenes to record.mjs and run it against the live site. Watch every scene.
  • VoiceSet up Kokoro on a short folder path. Test one line, then check the sounds of every line that has a name or an abbreviation.
  • CheckConfirm timing, audio levels and captions, and test playback on the page.
  • ReviewA person watches it with sound on and with sound off. Marketing checks brand and wording.
  • MusicChoose a track from its license and its Content ID status, keep the certificate, and build a music version with add-music.sh. Keep the no-music version so reviewers can compare.
  • PublishUpload the chosen version with the captions file, and say in the description that the voice is AI-generated.
  • MaintainRe-record when the pages shown change, and check it before each term.

9. Open items

  • Voiceover review. Nobody has listened to the Kokoro voice yet. The course lead needs to review voice, pronunciation and pacing.
  • Brand and wording sign-off. CityU marketing hasn't reviewed the draft.
  • Music or no music. Reviewers still need to compare the two versions. Nobody has heard the mix yet, so the balance is measured, not judged by ear.
  • Upload. CityU's channel owner uploads the chosen version with its captions file. A music version will probably get a Content ID claim, so marketing should agree that's acceptable first.
  • Upkeep. The video shows live pages, so changes to the student site will date it. Re-recording takes a machine with Chrome, Node, ffmpeg and the Kokoro install.
