AI 410 Revamp: New Grading and a Weekly Rhythm

The Muddiest Point is removed, the HOS absorbs the Application Challenge, Concept Debates and Knowledge Checks move to weekly, and the Term Project doubles to 40%. AI 410 goes first; AI 420, 430 and 440 copy it once it's final.

📅 Nov 17, 2026 ✅ 6 decisions made ❓ 2 still open 🧮 Still 1,000 points
BSAI reminder: the standing rules for AI 410 / 420 / 430 / 440 are in the course design constraints. This plan keeps all four: the official description and outcomes stay fixed, work stays in Codespaces on free tiers, and each HOS still spans two weeks (Weeks 1–8) with the Term Project in Weeks 9–10. The syllabus master stays Clark's OneDrive Word file; changes are drafted for him to apply there.

In short

  • Fewer, bigger graded pieces: HOS 40% (with the challenge inside it), Term Project 40%, Concept Debate 10%, Knowledge Check 10%.
  • Every week has the same small beat: one Concept Debate and one Knowledge Check. Week 1's check covers theory and Week 2's covers skills.
  • The name stays "HOS". Each HOS gets two named halves: Part A: Build (Week 1) and Part B: Apply (Week 2). The other names considered are kept below for the Dean.
  • Still open: how the 100 HOS points split between the build and the challenge, and whether the Dean prefers another name.

Splitting the 100 HOS points Open

Which split is fairest to students, and grading once at the end of Week 2

What "fair" means here

  • Weight follows the work. The build takes about a week and a half, the challenge about half a week. The old weighting was 100 : 50, so the challenge was about a third.
  • One mistake isn't punished twice. A broken build shouldn't sink the challenge too. The challenge has its own starter in challenge/, so it can be graded on its own.
  • Each skill is scored once. Analyze and Evaluate shouldn't be counted in both halves.
  • Students know the split up front and see it on the rubric.

The three options

OptionFairness to studentsGrading
A. 5 × 20, Apply = the challengeLeast fair. The challenge falls to 8% of the course grade. A weak build lowers four of the five criteria, so one mistake counts many times. The open design decision, worth 40% of the old AC, nearly disappears.Easiest: same shape as rubric 100981.
B. Build 60 / Challenge 40, overlaps trimmed LeanFairest. It stays close to the old one-third weighting while giving the individual transfer task real weight. Each half is scored on its own, so a broken build doesn't zero the challenge, and each skill is scored once.One rubric with two sections and seven criteria.
C. Build 50 / Challenge 50Over-weights the challenge, which is about a quarter of the time. Week 2 also holds the by-hand work on the build, so it isn't really one week per half.Simple to explain.

Levels stay at 100 / 90 / 75 / 60% of each criterion, as on every other AI 410 rubric. A 60/40 split doesn't depend on these exact criterion points; they can be tuned.

What if the instructor grades once, at the end of Week 2?

That already matches how HOS work is submitted: the last push to the Classroom50 repo before the end of Week 2 is what counts. Grading once works with any option. Option B still works well as one rubric with two sections, filled in at one sitting. The real trade-offs are about feedback, not points:

  • Pro: one grading pass per module (4 per student per term, not 8), and students get the whole second week to fix Week 1 problems before anything is scored.
  • Con: no instructor feedback in the middle of the module. A student who misunderstood the build in Week 1 may only find out after the module ends.
  • Con: work can pile up at the end of Week 2, because nothing forces a Week 1 checkpoint.
  • Mitigation, ungraded: the Week 1 theory Knowledge Check gives automatic feedback. The existing "End of Week 1" checklist on each lesson stays as a self-check, and commit history still shows whether Evaluate happened in Week 1.

Recommendation: option B, graded once at the end of Week 2, with the Week 1 Knowledge Check and self-check as the early feedback. This needs Clark's confirmation before the rubric is built.
