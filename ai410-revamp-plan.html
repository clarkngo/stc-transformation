AI 410 Revamp: New Grading and a Weekly Rhythm

The Muddiest Point is removed, the HOS and the Application Challenge become one AI Challenge (AIC) with a short Post Mortem, Concept Debates and Knowledge Checks move to weekly, and a 15-minute faculty interview takes 20% (since Nov 24; the Term Project stays at 20%). AI 410 goes first; AI 420, 430 and 440 copy it once it's final.

📅 Nov 17, 2026 · updated Nov 23 ✅ 12 decisions made ❓ 3 still open 🧮 Still 1,000 points
BSAI reminder: the standing rules for AI 410 / 420 / 430 / 440 are in the course design constraints. This plan keeps all four: the official description and outcomes stay fixed, work stays in Codespaces on free tiers, and each HOS still spans two weeks (Weeks 1–8) with the Term Project in Weeks 9–10. The syllabus master stays Clark's OneDrive Word file; changes are drafted for him to apply there.

In short

  • Fewer, bigger graded pieces: AI Challenge 40% (the old HOS with the challenge inside it), Term Project 20% plus a 20% Faculty Interview (changed Nov 24 from Term Project 40%), Concept Debate 10%, Knowledge Check 10%.
  • Every week has the same small beat: one Concept Debate and one Knowledge Check. Week 1's check covers theory and Week 2's covers skills.
  • New name (Nov 19): AI Challenge (AIC). Each AIC has two halves, Part A: Build (Week 1) and Part B: Apply (Week 2), and ends with a short Post Mortem that takes over the Muddiest Point's job. The other names considered are kept below.
  • AIC rubric: build 60 / challenge 40, graded once at the end of Week 2 (decided Nov 17). The Post Mortem is graded inside the Understand criterion, so the split doesn't change.
  • Knowledge Checks: best attempt counts (Nov 20): 5 questions × 2 points, unlimited attempts, and the highest attempt is the grade. This replaced the Nov 19 pass/fail rule, which Brightspace can't run without extra grade formulas. See below.
  • Closing the loop (Nov 23): the instructor answers the class's most common Post Mortem "still unclear" points at the start of the next module, and a short anonymous feedback survey after each AIC asks which activities helped. See progress.
  • Term Project interview (Nov 19): faculty interview each student about their project in Week 9 or 10. See below.
  • Where it stands (Nov 23): the site, template repos and Knowledge Checks are on v2.1. The rest of the Brightspace shell (gradebook, rubrics, discussions and content pages) is still v1. See progress.
  • Interview points (Nov 24): one 15-minute faculty interview worth 200 points (20%). The Term Project goes back to 20% (200 points), so the total stays 1,000. Drafted in syllabus v3; see the interview page.
  • Still open: approving the Debate B topics, and the KC questions from the Nov 3 review (attempts, data plan, timing wording).

Splitting the 100 AIC points Decided

Which split is fairest to students, and grading once at the end of Week 2

What "fair" means here

  • Weight follows the work. The build takes about a week and a half, the challenge about half a week. The old weighting was 100 : 50, so the challenge was about a third.
  • One mistake isn't punished twice. A broken build shouldn't sink the challenge too. The challenge has its own starter in challenge/, so it can be graded on its own.
  • Each skill is scored once. Analyze and Evaluate shouldn't be counted in both halves.
  • Students know the split up front and see it on the rubric.

The three options

OptionFairness to studentsGrading
A. 5 × 20, Apply = the challengeLeast fair. The challenge falls to 8% of the course grade. A weak build lowers four of the five criteria, so one mistake counts many times. The open design decision, worth 40% of the old AC, nearly disappears.Easiest: same shape as rubric 100981.
B. Build 60 / Challenge 40, overlaps trimmed ChosenFairest. It stays close to the old one-third weighting while giving the individual transfer task real weight. Each half is scored on its own, so a broken build doesn't zero the challenge, and each skill is scored once.One rubric with two sections and seven criteria.
C. Build 50 / Challenge 50Over-weights the challenge, which is about a quarter of the time. Week 2 also holds the by-hand work on the build, so it isn't really one week per half.Simple to explain.

The chosen split, option B in detail

Changed Nov 24: Scott Zhou's rubric weights. The 60/40 split stays, but the criteria inside each half now follow Scott's Nov 5 grids. Build 60: Create, Evaluate, Analyze, Apply and Understand at 12 points each (his 5 × 20%). Challenge 40: Application 4, Analysis 8, Evaluation 12, Design 16 (his 10 / 20 / 30 / 40%). Concept Debate: Participation 60 / Writing 40. Levels use his four bands (Exceeds 100–92, At 91–75, Approaching 74–62, Below 61–0). The table below is the Nov 17 version, kept for the record.

Levels stay at 100 / 90 / 75 / 60% of each criterion, as on every other AI 410 rubric. A 60/40 split doesn't depend on these exact criterion points; they can be tuned.

What if the instructor grades once, at the end of Week 2?

That already matches how HOS work is submitted: the last push to the Classroom50 repo before the end of Week 2 is what counts. Grading once works with any option. Option B still works well as one rubric with two sections, filled in at one sitting. The real trade-offs are about feedback, not points:

  • Pro: one grading pass per module (4 per student per term, not 8), and students get the whole second week to fix Week 1 problems before anything is scored.
  • Con: no instructor feedback in the middle of the module. A student who misunderstood the build in Week 1 may only find out after the module ends.
  • Con: work can pile up at the end of Week 2, because nothing forces a Week 1 checkpoint.
  • Mitigation, ungraded: the Week 1 theory Knowledge Check gives automatic feedback. The existing "End of Week 1" checklist on each lesson stays as a self-check, and commit history still shows whether Evaluate happened in Week 1.

Decision (Nov 17): option B, graded once at the end of Week 2, with the Week 1 Knowledge Check and self-check as the early feedback.

Post Mortem, in place of the Muddiest Point Decided

Should it be optional, and does it overlap what the AIC already asks?

Short answer: a full post mortem would mostly repeat work the AIC already grades. Only the part the Muddiest Point used to do is new. So the Post Mortem is short and required (three prompts, about 150 words), not optional, and it skips the parts that would overlap.

What already covers "what went wrong"

A typical post-mortem questionAlready asked byOverlap
What broke, and why?Evaluate (tests and failures) and Analyze (the mechanism, failure modes)Full
Which design choices did you make, and were they right?The challenge's Evaluation criterion (Justify until Nov 24) (DECISIONS.md / REPORT.md)Full
What do you understand now that you didn't?Understand (your own explanation of the by-hand change)Partial
What is still unclear to you?Nothing since the Muddiest Point was removedNone: this is the new part
What would you do differently next time, including how you used the AI assistant?NothingNone: this is the new part

Why required, not optional

  • An optional reflection is skipped most by the students who are most lost, and they are the ones the instructor needs to hear from.
  • Short and specific keeps it from turning into busywork. It is graded inside the Understand criterion (12 points since Nov 24; 10 before), so the 60/40 split and the 1,000-point total don't change.

The three prompts

  1. Still unclear: the one idea from this module you'd least want to explain to someone else, and why. (This is the old Muddiest Point.)
  2. Next time: one thing you'd do differently, in the build or in how you used the AI assistant.
  3. Carry forward: one thing from this AIC you'll reuse in your Term Project.

It goes under a "Post Mortem" heading in NOTES.md, written without AI, and is pushed with the rest of the AIC by the end of Week 2. The instructor reads prompt 1 across the class before the next module, and reteaches what comes up most.

If you'd rather keep it optional: drop it from the Understand criterion and label it ungraded. Nothing else changes. Expect most students to skip it.

Knowledge Checks: best attempt counts Decided

Weekly, 10 points each, unlimited attempts. Changed Nov 20 from pass/fail.
  • Score: each check has 5 questions worth 2 points, 10 in all. The grade is the student's highest attempt, so 8 of 10 earns 8.
  • Attempts are unlimited, with hint-only feedback. Students are told to retake it until they have all 10 points.
  • Timing: 8 minutes per attempt, due the Sunday of its week. Week 1 of a module covers theory, Week 2 covers skills.

Why it fits: the KC is practice in recalling ideas, not a ranking. Unlimited attempts with the best attempt counting means every student can reach full marks by the due date.

Why not pass/fail (decided Nov 19, changed Nov 20): the first plan gave 10 points for 80% or better and 0 below it. Brightspace can only do that with a formula grade item per check, and formula items don't count toward the final grade in a points-based gradebook. The other choice was converting scores by hand at each due date. Clark chose the version any instructor can run with no scripts or manual steps.

Faculty interview, Week 9 or 10 Decided

Each student has a short one-on-one interview with faculty about their Term Project, in Week 9 or Week 10 (about 15 minutes, live or on a video call). The student walks through their system and answers questions about how it works and why it was built that way. The interview checks that the student understands, and can defend, the work they submit, which matters more now that the Term Project is 40% of the grade.

Decided Nov 24 What the interview counts toward: its own 200 points (20%), scored with a four-level rubric and mapped to CO1. The Term Project's team deliverables go back to their v1 points (200), so the Term Project is 20% again. Format, rubric, question bank and prep guides: AI 410 Faculty Interview.
