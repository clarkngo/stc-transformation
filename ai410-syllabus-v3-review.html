AI 410 Syllabus v3: Scott's Committee Review Meets the Revamp

Scott Zhou's Nov 5 version, written after his meeting with the Curriculum Committee, was based on the v1 syllabus. Our v2 draft was built without it. The v3 draft starts from Scott's file and keeps his rubrics. It then layers on the revamp (AI Challenge, weekly debates and checks, Term Project 40%) and adds the course outcome table the committee asked for.

📅 Nov 24, 2026 📝 v3 = tracked changes on Scott's file 🎯 16 assignments mapped to 5 COs ❓ 5 decisions for Clark
BSAI reminder: the standing rules for AI 410 / 420 / 430 / 440 are in the course design constraints. One conflict to flag: rule 1 says the official course description and outcomes are fixed, but Scott's file rewrites the description and trims the outcome verbs (for example, "Define and explain" becomes "Explain"). v3 keeps Scott's wording. If it is the committee-approved text, it becomes the new fixed version, and the constraints page and course pages need to match it.

In short

  • Scott rebuilt every rubric as a four-level grid (Exceeds 100–92%, At 91–75%, Approaching 74–62%, Below 61–0%). Each criterion has its own descriptor. Ours had one line per criterion and no levels. Side by side below.
  • His weights differ from ours in three places (Nov 24: we follow his): the HOS build (5 × 20% vs our 10/15/15/10/10), the challenge (4 criteria at 10/20/30/40 vs our Design 20 / Justify 20), and the Concept Debate (60/40 vs our 50/50). v3 uses Scott's.
  • v3 keeps everything Scott removed out of the syllabus: the course schedule, the HOS and AC module descriptions, the Term Project phase and deliverable details, and the decimal grade table. The rubrics still refer to some of those details, though. See the list.
  • Course outcome alignment (new): each assignment maps to the one outcome it assesses most directly. Hands-on work maps to the practical COs and conceptual work to CO1 and CO3, per today's meeting. Table below, also inserted into v3.
  • One AIC rubric (decided Nov 25): v3 merges Scott's HOS and Application Challenge rubrics into one AI Challenge rubric of nine criteria weighted as % of the grade like Scott's tables: Build Creation, Evaluation, Analysis, Apply and Understand at 12% each, then Challenge Application 4%, Analysis 8%, Evaluation 12% and Design 16%, on his four-level grid. The separate Application Challenge section is deleted. Same criteria and weights as before; it now matches the one rubric attached to each AIC grade item in Brightspace.
  • Interview (decided Nov 24, folded into the Term Project Nov 25): one 15-minute individual interview, 200 points, mapped to CO1, with its own four-level rubric as the last part of the Term Project section in v3. The Term Project is 40%: team deliverables at their v1 points (200) plus the Interview (200). Interview page.
  • BSAI PDG 2026: AI 410–440 aren't named in columns G and H, so none of them is a program-level assessment point. AI 410's Final Report still uses the same rubric rows. Details.

From today's meeting (Nov 24)

Curriculum Committee (CC) and outcome alignment
  • Strict CO mapping: the CC wants each assignment mapped to specific course outcomes. An assignment shouldn't claim to cover them all. Hands-on activities align with practical COs; conceptual activities align with theoretical COs.
  • Mapping table: an explicit CO mapping table in the syllabus helps the CC review go faster.

Conflict with Scott's file: his Term Project heading lists CO1–CO5 and his HOS heading lists four COs. That is the "covers everything" pattern the CC asked us to avoid. v3 moves the COs onto each Term Project deliverable and each AIC module.

BSAI PDG 2026: where AI 410 fits

Columns G (course) and H (assessment) of the BSAI sheet

The PDG assigns each program outcome rubric row to one course and assessment, such as IS 312 / TP03 Final Report or CS 340 / CD10 Concept Debate. None of AI 410, 420, 430 or 440 appears in column G. So their activities carry no program-level assessment duty. They should still teach toward the same outcomes and, where it's natural, reuse the same rubric rows. AI 410 already does:

Two things to raise with Scott about the sheet: (1) in rows 37–56 (PO6–PO8) the levels run backwards. The "Exceeds" column holds the weakest descriptor (for example, "Completes few assigned tasks…") and "Below" holds the strongest.
