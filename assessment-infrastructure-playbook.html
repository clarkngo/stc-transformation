Why: Content Teaches, Assessment Proves

The feature employers say they cannot find elsewhere

"Assessment infrastructure" is the systems, people, and processes that let CityU prove a learner is competent — as opposed to content, which only teaches. A credential is worth what its assessment is worth. Everything below turns "we taught them AI literacy" into "we can show, with evidence an employer trusts, that this officer can do X."

The evidence review found that employers value credentials that are transcripted, competency-based, and verifiable — and that a certificate of attendance is dismissed. Assessment is what moves a CityU credential from the second category into the first. It is also the binding constraint: it takes longer to build than content and is the reason "three rigorous credentials beat fifteen shallow ones."

Definition of done for any credential: not shippable until its competencies, performance tasks, validated rubric, and rater calibration all exist. Content without this is a workshop, not a credential.
🧭
STEP 1

Map Competencies from the Job

You cannot assess competence you have not defined observably

Run a job/task analysis with employer SMEs — for this credential, SPD supervisors and command staff, not CityU faculty. The DACUM method (a facilitated session where practitioners enumerate the actual tasks of the role) is the standard tool; the agenda for exactly this session is at the bottom of this page.

Write each competency as an observable performance statement — a performance verb + a condition/context + a standard. Knowledge statements ("understands X") are not assessable.

✕ Not assessable Understands the risk of AI hallucination in report writing.
✓ Assessable Given an AI-drafted incident report, identifies hallucinated or unsupported content and corrects it in line with SPD policy.

Worked example — competency map (AI for First Responders)

STEP 2

Design Performance Tasks

Authentic work, not multiple-choice recall

For each competency, build a task that mirrors the real job — a work-sample, a case simulation, a portfolio artifact, or a capstone with a real deliverable. The realism is the differentiator; a quiz on AI facts is exactly what Coursera already sells.

  • Anchor in real, redacted artifacts from the partner — actual (de-identified) report drafts, real policy edge cases, real vendor one-pagers to critique.
  • One task can cover several competencies. A single "review this AI-drafted report" task can hit FR-2, FR-4, and FR-5 at once — don't build one task per competency mechanically.
  • Make the deliverable the kind of thing the job produces — a corrected report with a change log, a risk classification memo, a one-paragraph escalation note.
Watch the confidentiality line: real SPD artifacts must be redacted/de-identified before use, cleared with the partner. Build a small library of reusable, cleared scenarios so you are not sourcing fresh material every cohort.
📐
STEP 3

Build & Validate Rubrics

This step produces the "employer-validated" claim

Write an analytic rubric per task: criteria rows × performance levels, each cell with a concrete behavioral descriptor. Then run a calibration (range-finding) session where several raters independently score the same sample responses, compare, and rewrite fuzzy descriptors until they converge. Capture the SMEs' sign-off — it validates the rubric, is evidence for accreditors, and is the receipt behind the marketing claim.

Worked example — rubric for FR-2 (correct AI-hallucinated content)

Criterion 1 · Not yet 2 · Approaching 3 · Competent 4 · Exemplary
Detection Misses most fabricated/unsupported claims. Flags some; misses subtle or plausible-sounding fabrications. Flags all material hallucinations and unsupported claims. Flags all, and distinguishes hallucination from ambiguity vs. genuine gaps in source.
Correction Edits introduce new errors or remove valid content. Corrects flagged items but leaves some inaccuracies. Corrections are accurate and grounded in the source record. Corrections are accurate, minimal, and preserve the officer's own voice/intent.
Policy alignment Ignores or contradicts SPD policy. Partially applies policy; misapplies at least one requirement. Applies the relevant SPD policy correctly throughout. Applies policy and cites the specific provision for each correction.
Documentation No record of what was changed or why. Notes changes but not rationale or source. Change log records what changed, why, and the source basis. Change log is disclosure-ready and would withstand prosecutor scrutiny.

Passing standard: set it explicitly with the SMEs — e.g., "Competent (3) or higher on every criterion; Detection may not fall below 3." Record the standard, not just the rubric.

The sign-off artifact is the deliverable. A one-page memo — "SPD SMEs reviewed and approved these competencies, tasks, rubrics, and passing standards on [date]" — is simultaneously your quality evidence, your accreditation evidence, and your license to say "employer-validated" in marketing.
⚖️
STEP 4

Make Scoring Reliable

A rubric means nothing if two assessors disagree

  • Anchor papers. Pick real sample responses that exemplify each performance level; assessors train against them.
  • Double-scoring. Two assessors independently score a sample (e.g., 20%) of submissions.
  • Adjudication. Where scores differ by more than one level, a third rater resolves it — and that disagreement tells you which descriptor is still fuzzy.
  • Track inter-rater reliability over time; if it drifts, re-calibrate. This is the difference between "competency-based" and vibes with a rubric attached.
Reuse across credentials. The rater-training pattern, anchor-paper format, and adjudication rule are credential-agnostic — build them once and every future credential inherits them.

STEP 5

Identity & Integrity — "Verifiable Delivery"

Match the integrity method to the stakes; don't over-engineer

Low stakes
Formative practice

Auto-graded practice, self-check, peer review. No proctoring needed — these build skill, they don't certify it.

Medium stakes
Performance tasks

Verified-track identity at enrollment. Authorship confirmed by a short oral defense / viva of the submitted work — better proof than lockdown software.

High stakes
Capstone / knowledge exam

Live capstone presentation (authorship + competence in one) and/or proctored exam where a timed knowledge check genuinely warrants it.

Verifiable issuance

Issue the credential as an Open Badge 3.0 / verifiable credential carrying metadata: issuer, the specific competencies demonstrated, assessment type, date, and credit value. That metadata is what lets an employer actually check the credential — which is the entire point of "verifiable."

For performance-based credentials, authorship is the real concern — not exam-room lockdown. A live defense verifies both that the learner did the work and that they understand it. Reserve heavy proctoring for the narrow case of a high-stakes timed knowledge test.
🔌
STEP 6

Wire Into the Platform & Governance

Open edX features + registrar sign-off

  • Open Response Assessments (ORA) — Open edX's rubric-scored, staff/peer/self-graded task type. This is where the performance tasks and analytic rubrics live.
  • Verified certificates — identity verification for medium/high-stakes assessment.
  • Proctoring integration — enable only for the narrow high-stakes exam case.
  • Transcripting & articulation — the registrar and faculty governance must approve credit and the articulation agreement, or the credential is not stackable and CityU loses its differentiator. Start this in parallel, not after.
Sequencing trap: teams build all the content, then discover the credential can't be transcripted. Get the articulation path approved before launch, alongside assessment design.

Minimum Viable Version

For AI for First Responders — shippable in weeks, not quarters

  • 1
    6–10 competencies from one SPD job-analysis session (agenda below).
  • 2
    2–3 performance tasks + 1 capstone, built on redacted real scenarios.
  • 3
    Analytic rubrics validated in one calibration session with SPD SMEs — with the sign-off memo captured.
  • 4
    Capstone defended live — that is your authorship check; no proctoring software required.
  • 5
    Issued as a verifiable credential listing the demonstrated competencies, with the credit/articulation path approved.
Every piece here is reusable: the rater-training pattern, rubric format, integrity tiers, and issuance setup carry straight into the next credential (maritime OT security, healthcare AI governance). You build the infrastructure once and amortize it.

SPD Job-Analysis Session Agenda

The concrete first step — a half-day DACUM-style workshop to produce the competency map

Before the session

  • Room: 5–8 SPD practitioners across levels (officers, supervisors, command), 1 facilitator (CityU), 1 scribe. Practitioners, not just leadership — the people who do the work.
  • Pre-work: ask each to bring 2–3 recent real situations where AI touched their work (report drafting, review, a vendor pitch). De-identify in advance.
  • Frame it honestly: "We're defining what an officer should be able to do with AI, so we can build a credential that proves it — not selling you a tool."
