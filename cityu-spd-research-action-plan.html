Working Thesis

What we now know changes the pitch

Don't lead with
Time savings on report narratives. An independent, pre-registered RCT (Adams et al., 2024) found no significant reduction in report-writing time from Axon's Draft One — directly contradicting vendor marketing. SPD's own senior research scientist raised this study unprompted, which means SPD's own research arm is already skeptical of the efficiency pitch. Leading with "2 hours → 60 seconds" undercuts our credibility with the one audience whose trust we need most.
Lead with instead
What SPD is already doing: positioning AI as QA, consistency, and supervisory accountability tooling (Mark 43 prompts, planned NLP review of reasonable-suspicion language) rather than a narrative-writing shortcut. This is also the approach least likely to trip the King County Prosecutor's blanket prohibition, since it never puts AI output into a signed narrative.
CityU's actual opening
Not "build us an AI report writer." It's research partner, third-party evaluator, and education provider — three roles SPD explicitly said it doesn't have covered (no evaluation partner since its prior candidate lost funding; no internal capacity to run the kind of mixed tech + social-science evaluation SPD described wanting).

Four Workstreams

Mapped from the team's "four-headed dragon" framework

🔬 Research

Re-run or replicate the report-writing speed question with current-generation models (the 2024 study is already dated). Investigate CJIS-data handling patterns at peer agencies. Document SPD's QA-first AI use cases as a case study.

Lead: Clark, Sam Chung
🧪 Evaluation

Serve as SPD's third-party evaluator for AI tools entering production (CaseX/Corvus, Mark 43 QA, closed Claude instance) — combining technologists and social scientists so findings aren't dismissed as either purely technical or purely advocacy-driven. Support SPD's ISO certification goal for the closed Claude deployment.

Lead: CityU eval team + AI strategy consultant
🎓 Education

Workshops and micro-credentials for officers, supervisors, and command staff on AI literacy. Separate track for policy-level education aimed at the prosecutor's office and other skeptical stakeholders — not advocacy, just shared vocabulary (e.g., distinguishing "transcription" from "AI" from "generative AI").

Lead: AI strategy consultant, Sam Chung
📣 Advocacy / Integration

Help SPD socialize a differentiated narrative to the prosecutor and city stakeholders: QA/accountability tooling first, narrative generation deferred. Draft MOU language for the CityU–SPD research relationship. Scope whether the same model extends to SFD/EMS.

Lead: CityU partnerships lead, SPD partnerships lead

CityU's Evaluator Role — Scope

SPD said it doesn't have this. We can be it.

Why us
SPD's prior evaluation-partner candidate lost funding last year. SPD's research lead explicitly named the gap. CityU's applied-research model (vs. purely peer-reviewed academic output) was specifically called out by SPD leadership as a differentiator worth pursuing.
Composition
Mixed team of technologists and social scientists — CityU was explicit that tech-only evaluation invites bias critiques the team can't defend against alone.
What gets evaluated
  • Tools as they're adopted: CaseX/Corvus, Mark 43 QA prompts, the planned NLP supervisor-review model
  • The closed Claude instance, as it moves from low-risk to medium/high-risk use cases
  • Progress against SPD's ISO certification goal (NIST is the fallback standard, ISO is SPD's preferred standard)
Adjacent ask
The team's AI strategy consultant, as a CityU-affiliated partner, could conduct structured interviews with internal skeptics — starting with the prosecutor's office — to surface the actual root objection rather than guessing at it.

Prosecutor Strategy

The blanket AI prohibition is the central blocker — treat it as political, not technical

Diagnosis
Group consensus on the call: the prohibition is preemptive of public concern, not a response to a specific incident. Root cause still unconfirmed — needs to be established before any messaging is built.
Approach
  • Don't lecture the prosecutor's office — bring them along.
  • Separate "AI" from "technology" in the conversation. A lot of the QA/transcription work isn't generative AI at all; conflating them invites unnecessary opposition.
  • Demonstrate trust incrementally with already-running tools (Mark 43, CaseX/Corvus) before reopening the narrative-writing question.
  • Use the AI strategy consultant's interviews (see above) to find the actual lever — is it case-loss risk, liability exposure, or general caution?
Reference point
See ref-7 in the Research Brief for the EFF coverage of the underlying King County PA memo, and the brief's broader framing of why AI-as-QA is more defensible than AI-as-author.
