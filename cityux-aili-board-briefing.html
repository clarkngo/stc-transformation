Target Personas

Not "everyone" — three concrete people the funnel is actually built for
Why three, and why these three: "everyone" isn't a target. Each persona below maps to a real touchpoint already live in the ecosystem — not a hypothetical audience we'd need to go build something new for.
😟
The Anxious Achiever
Aisha, 31 — marketing associate

Reads an AI headline every morning and isn't sure her job title survives five more years. Not technical, doesn't want to become an engineer — just wants enough fluency to not get left behind in her own field.

Typically enters via → The Ebook-First Lead
🛠️
The Self-Taught Fixer
Jordan, 24 — works the front desk

The person everyone in the office asks to fix their laptop. Never thought of himself as "a tech person" because he never did well in school — a CS degree has never once occurred to him as something for people like him.

Typically enters via → Discovered via Industry Practitioner Content
🌱
The Deferred-Dream Learner
Marisol, 49 — started an associate's degree at 19

Work, then kids, then more work — the degree never got finished, but the door never fully closed either. Not intimidated by the material; tired of feeling like she missed her window. Needs the on-ramp to feel judgment-free.

Typically enters via → The Community-First Explorer

User Stories & Journeys

The funnel isn't one path — six learners, six entry points, six different "wins"
🎥
The Self-Directed Learner
Organic discovery via search or algorithm recommendation
TOFU · YouTube (official or practitioner-published) → MOFU · GitHub + Open edX → BOFU · Community Hub + Workshop → Conversion · Program registration

The "default" path the ecosystem is built around — most predictable journey to design touchpoints and nurture sequences around. Covers content from an official CityU channel or an individual practitioner-publisher, as long as that content routes into Open edX.

🎙️
Discovered via Industry Practitioner Content
Finds CityUX indirectly — through a practitioner's own YouTube/blog/GitHub content, not an official CityU channel
TOFU · Practitioner-published content → Branches by platform

An individual practitioner builds trust under their own name; if their content routes into Open edX it collapses into the Self-Directed Learner path above, and if it doesn't (yet), it follows a compressed path straight to Conversion — real example, today: STRIDE for AI Agents, a self-hosted GitHub Pages course.

Persona fit → Jordan, The Self-Taught Fixer
🚔
The Frontline Professional
e.g. an SPD officer or chief, reached through an institutional partnership
BOFU · Workshop (skill-up cohort) → MOFU · Open edX compliance modules → Conversion · Department-recognized credential

Skips TOFU discovery entirely — the department partnership is the entry point. "Conversion" here means a workplace-recognized micro-credential or compliance certificate, not CityU degree enrollment.

💬
The Community-First Explorer
Finds CityUX through word of mouth, a starred GitHub repo, or a Discord invite from a peer
BOFU · Community Hub → MOFU · Open edX + GitHub (backfilled) → Conversion · Micro-credential or program interest

Enters "out of order" — proof the funnel isn't strictly linear. Real example, live today: the CityUX Discord has dedicated #ebooks and #courses channels routing members back into the ebook and course touchpoints.

Persona fit → Marisol, The Deferred-Dream Learner
📘
The Ebook-First Lead
Discovers CityUX through a promoted ebook — a LinkedIn post, seminar handout, or workshop follow-up — before ever touching a course
TOFU · LinkedIn / social post → MOFU · Gated ebook download → BOFU · Nurture email + Community Hub invite → Conversion · Program registration or partnership conversation

The one journey where identity is captured at the first touchpoint, not the last — a real email, immediately. Real example, live today: CityU Guides, three gated ebooks already published (AI skills, first responders, career changers).

Persona fit → Aisha, The Anxious Achiever
🌏
The Prospective International Student
Reached via an institutional partnership abroad, culminating in an in-person visit — not just outreach
TOFU · Institutional partnership (e.g. iKW, Korea) → MOFU · Open edX pilot → BOFU · In-person Seattle visit → Conversion · Formal international enrollment

Highest-value outcome — full degree enrollment, not a micro-credential — but the longest cycle. Real example: the iKW Summer Program — Korean students traveled to Seattle for an in-person cohort, coursework delivered on the same Open edX instance used across this ecosystem.

TOFU → MOFU → BOFU — Quick Explainer

In case a board member asks "what does that mean?"
TOFU · Discovery (YouTube) → MOFU · Hands-on (GitHub + Open edX) → BOFU · High-touch (Workshops, Seminars, Community Hub) → Conversion · Formal CityU enrollment
Top of Funnel
TOFU

Broad, low-commitment discovery — a YouTube video, a LinkedIn post, a gated ebook. The visitor doesn't know CityU yet.

Middle of Funnel
MOFU

Hands-on engagement — a GitHub repo, a self-paced Open edX pilot. The learner is now actively trying something, not just watching.

Bottom of Funnel
BOFU

High-touch validation — a live workshop, a seminar, the Community Hub. Real people, real feedback, real relationship.

Conversion
Enrollment

Credit articulation, direct program registration, or a faculty/advisory touchpoint — the learner becomes a CityU student.

CityUX (Working Title) — Org Structure

AI is a specialty inside CityUX, not the name of the whole thing
Naming is still open. "CityUX" is a working title for the umbrella Institute-Level Entity (ILE) — the alternative on the table is "Institute for Cyber & Emerging Technologies." Either way, the shape is the same: the umbrella covers multiple specialties, and AILI (the AI Lifelong Institute) is one specialty underneath it — not the top-level name — so cybersecurity, quantum, and future tracks can join later without a rebrand.
Umbrella level
The Institute-Level Entity reports to the President's Office directly, holds its own budget, and shares a joint steering committee with STC and other university leadership — not a "Chief of Staff" reporting line.

Open Source & IP Model

Who can contribute, what tooling is used, and who owns what
Who can contribute
Not limited to CityU faculty. Any CityU school, any outside practitioner with an idea worth teaching, and partner nonprofits can develop and pitch course content through the same pipeline.
Delivery & assessment tooling
Open edX stays the core platform — free, open-source, doesn't require a CityU email, and supports bring-your-own-course content.
Content ownership — open by default
Nobody owns course content individually — it's open-source. Authors declare originality when they submit it; anyone downstream can reuse it as long as they give attribution back to the original author. License: Creative Commons Attribution (CC BY).
Compliance exception
Tracks with a genuine compliance need (e.g. law-enforcement or regulatory content) can declare ownership instead of releasing under the open license — the open-by-default rule isn't absolute where it would create legal exposure.
Internal service-fee model
When another CityU school wants training, seminar delivery, or infrastructure, it's billed as an internal service fee — not a grant or a favor. This is what lets the model scale across schools without every request becoming a governance conversation.

Practitioner-Instructors & Content Developers

Industry credibility in front of the camera, instructional support behind it
Delivery Layer
Practitioner-Instructors & Content Developers
How the content that fuels the funnel actually gets made
Industry leaders and chiefs serve as instructors in the asynchronous model
Instructional content developers support behind the scenes — scripting, video, quiz design
Sourced through STC's existing practitioner-publisher pipeline
Open beyond CityU staff — outside practitioners pitching their own course idea, and partner nonprofits developing content, enter through this same pipeline
The STC Liaison sources practitioner-instructors specifically for the Maritime OT and AI-literacy tracks
The supply side is already running. Industry Pro, Public Voice is a live course that trains CityU instructors who are already industry professionals to turn what they already teach into public content — video, writing, talks — that builds their own reputation and CityU's visibility, on a time budget that respects their day job. This is the mechanism that produces the "Discovered via Industry Practitioner Content" journey from Part 1, rather than leaving it to chance.

CityUX / AILI — Services & Revenue

What it actually sells, to whom, and what it costs to run
External
  • Continuing education
  • Industry certification
  • Industry license
  • Digital badge (per-seminar credential)
  • Long-term: e-commerce marketplace — B2C first, then B2B
Internal
  • Other schools buy/license an existing course rather than building their own
  • Learner data passed to the marketing team
  • Service-based — internal fee when another school draws on this capacity
To Support
  • Infrastructure (Open edX hosting, tooling)
  • Training for instructors
  • Training for authors
Naming: seminar, not workshop. The delivery format is branded a seminar going forward — "workshop" stays reserved for internal planning sessions. The SPD and Maritime frontline tracks ship under one external brand, The Frontline Professional, so officers and port/maritime workers see a single, recognizable series.
Top-of-funnel: YouTube to course. Short-form content on YouTube and social media is the entry point — anybody with an idea worth selling pitches a topic, it gets tested as a video first, and only the ones that land turn into a full seminar or course. Same TOFU → MOFU → BOFU logic as Part 2 above — this is a content source feeding that funnel, not a separate one.
Owning the marketplace, not renting it. The long-term marketplace runs on CDUX.edu (official domain), with a CDUX.com floated for the paid marketplace phase — positioned so CityU owns the customer relationship directly, rather than depending on a Coursera-style platform that keeps it.
