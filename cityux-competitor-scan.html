1 · Five findings

The short version for stakeholders
1
Both lead audiences are already being trained for free, at scale, by the biggest AI companies. Anthropic, Google, Microsoft and OpenAI all have free AI courses for teachers or job seekers, often with a certificate. The teachers' unions are in it too: the AFT's academy, funded by Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic, aims to train 400,000 teachersK-12 Dive, and the U.S. Department of Labor runs a free AI course by text messageDOL. Plan B was right not to sell the basics.
2
"AI Fluency" is already Anthropic's course name. Anthropic offers AI Fluency for Educators and AI Fluency for pK–12 Educators, the second built with Teach For AmericaAnthropic. A CityUx course called "AI Fluency for Educators" would look like a copy of a free one.
3
For Washington teachers, AI clock hours are already cheap and close by. Educational service districts sell short AI courses for about $3 a clock hourESD 113, and WEA grants 15 clock hours for each NEA micro-credential, including a set of AI onesWEA. Clock hours alone won't justify a $249 cohort.
4
The clearest gap is for career changers: proof of skill that a person checks, tied to a degree path and to local employers. The free job-seeker courses end in a completion certificate. The one free assessed option we found, Microsoft Applied Skills, is an automated lab test of Microsoft's own toolsMicrosoft. None of them offers college credit.
5
CityUx doesn't need to build its own free core. Good free beginner courses already exist. Plan B could point learners to them and spend its money only on what nobody else offers: the human-scored credential, the coached cohort, and a credit path into CityU.

6 · Where CityUx can win, and where it can't

Being honest about each audience

Educators: a thinner gap than we thought

Free courses, cheap ESD clock hours and NEA micro-credentials with human review already cover most of what Plan B's educator lens offers. Selling $249 seats to individual teachers will be hard.

What's still open: expert facilitation for a whole school or district (what RAND's district leaders couldn't find), and credit toward a CityU education degree or endorsement, which no one else offers. That points to district contracts and a credit path, not individual seats.

Career changers: a real gap

Every free job-seeker option ends with a completion certificate or an automated lab test. None has a person score the learner's own work, none grants college credit, and few connect to local employers.

What CityUx can offer: a $99 human-scored task from the job the learner wants, a short live defense, credit toward a CityU program, and referrals from WorkSource and Goodwill, which already run the free introductions.

Partner where others are strong. WEA and the NEA micro-credentials, the ESDs, Goodwill, and the workforce boards that Microsoft is already funding all reach our audiences. CityUx can be the next step after their free courses rather than another first course.

7 · What this changes in Plan B

Proposals for the CityUx team to decide on
  • 1
    Rename the course. "AI Fluency for X" collides with Anthropic's free courses of the same name. Pick a CityUx name that says what's different, for example something built around "proof" or "applied AI".
  • 2
    Curate the free core instead of building it. Point learners to the best free courses (for example Anthropic's, Common Sense's, and the DOL framework) and map them to the CityUx credential. That would cut most of the $20,000 Plan B set aside for building its own four-module core, and lets the team start on the credential sooner.
  • 3
    Lead the sales pitch with the credential and credit, not the course. What nobody else offers is a person scoring your own work plus credit toward a CityU program. Ask the Registrar and School of Education early whether that credit is possible, because it is the strongest differentiator.
  • 4
    Reprice the educator offer, or sell it to districts. Against $3 clock hours and $186–$249 ISTE courses, test a district cohort price and a credit-bearing option rather than $249 per teacher.
  • 5
    Add three questions to the Gate 0 interviews: which free AI courses they've already taken, what they got from them, and what would make a credential worth more than the certificate they already have.
  • 6
    Talk to WEA and Goodwill in Phase 0. Both already reach our audiences with free AI training and could refer learners to the assessed credential.
Adopted Nov 25: all six changes are now in Plan B and the Gate 0 plan, with the working name Applied AI for X. Plan B's year-one costs fell from $79,000 to $63,000.
