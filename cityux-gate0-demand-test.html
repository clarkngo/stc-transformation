1 · The test in one page

Ten weeks, two audiences, one decision

Plan B gives away the AI Fluency for X course and charges for a human-scored credential ($99), a coached cohort ($249) and private cohorts. Before anything is built, Gate 0 checks one thing: will people in our two lead audiences commit time or money now? If they won't, we stop after ten weeks and about $8,000, not after a $45,000 course build.

2
audiences tested: K-12 educators and career changers
30
interviews, 15 per audience
3
free 90-minute workshops, each ending with the reservation offer
$25
refundable seat reservation, the test's main signal
Pass
An audience passes with 20 or more paid reservations, or a signed letter of intent from a school district, state agency or employer. The audience gets its X lens built in Phase 1 (March–June 2027).
Extend
An audience with 150 or more waitlist sign-ups but too few reservations gets one 4-week follow-up test, not a build. A waitlist costs people nothing, so it isn't enough on its own.
Stop
Everything else stops. Reservations are refunded, the waitlist is told the outcome, and we write up what we learned.
This is a little stricter than Plan B as written. Plan B lets 150 waitlist sign-ups count as a pass on their own. A free sign-up is a weak signal, so this page treats it as a reason to keep testing rather than a reason to build. The CityUx team should agree to these rules before Dec 7, so the results can't move the goalposts.

2 · Decide before Dec 7

Four things that block the test if they aren't settled
  • 1
    A way to take $25 reservations. Ask CityU Finance how the university takes small payments for non-credit events, and whether they can be refunded easily. This is the biggest blocker. Fallback: a free reservation for a named cohort date with a signed commitment. That is a weaker signal, so it would need 40 reservations instead of 20 to pass.
  • 2
    Permission to email CityU lists. School of Education alumni and STC and CityU alumni are the first two channels. Ask Marketing and Alumni Relations what can be sent, how often, and under whose name.
  • 3
    A School of Education partner. One named faculty member who co-owns the educator test, reviews the copy and helps with interviews. Ask whether CityU already issues OSPI clock hours. If it does, the educator lens can use that approval rather than applying from scratch.
  • 4
    Agreement on the gate rules. The CityUx team signs off on Section 9 before anything goes live.

3 · What we need to learn

Each question is tied to a piece of evidence we will collect

K-12 educators

  1. Will they commit? Reservations for a spring cohort and workshop turnout.
  2. Do clock hours matter? Compare sign-ups on two versions of the page, with and without "earn clock hours", and ask in interviews how they chose their last clock-hour course.
  3. Who pays? The teacher, the district, or a grant? Interviews, plus at least two conversations with district professional-development leads.
  4. What do they want AI for? Lesson prep, feedback, communication with families, or student use policy? This shapes the lens.

Career changers

  1. Will they commit? Reservations and workshop turnout, as for educators.
  2. Is $99 for proof believable? Interviews ask what training they have paid for in the last year and why.
  3. Which subgroup responds? Alumni, office and admin staff, workers over 50, or recent grads. The sign-up form asks, so the counts can be split.
  4. Would an employer value the credential? Three or four short calls with hiring managers in CityU's employer network.

Also in Phase 0, but not part of the pass/fail count: 2–3 meetings with state agency training managers about how they meet WaTech's AI training requirement; one interactive 3D teaching scene tried out in the workshops; and a size estimate for each audience. A state agency letter of intent would still count as a pass.

4 · What we put in front of people

Almost nothing is built: two pages, a form, three workshops and one offer

Two landing pages

Short pages on the CityUx site, one per audience. Each page has a headline, three bullets, an outline of the four modules, and two buttons: Join the waitlist (free) and Reserve a spring seat ($25). Draft copy:

Educators

AI Fluency for Educators
Use AI well in the work you already do: planning, feedback and talking with families. Built with CityU's School of Education.

  • Free, self-paced course, about 6 hours
  • Optional coached cohort this spring, 4 live weeks
  • Clock hours planned (pending approval)
Career changers

AI Fluency for Your Next Job
Show employers you can use AI on real work, not just talk about it. From City University of Seattle.

  • Free, self-paced course, about 6 hours
  • $99 credential: a real task from your field, scored by a person
  • Free or reduced cost if you're out of work

Say "planned" or "pending" for anything that doesn't exist yet. People are reserving a seat in a course that will be built only if enough of them do, and the page should say so plainly.

The sign-up form

One form for both pages, with a hidden field for which page it came from. Fields: name, email, role (with subgroup choices for career changers), school district or employer (optional), ZIP code, how you heard about us, and one question: "Would you pay $99 for a CityU credential that proves you can use AI at work?" (yes, maybe, no).

The reservation offer

$25 holds a seat in the spring coached cohort. It is credited toward the $249 cohort price, or fully refunded on request at any time before the cohort starts, or automatically if the cohort doesn't run. Unemployed learners can reserve for free by checking a box; those reservations are counted separately.

7 · Interview guides

20–30 minutes. Ask about what people have done, not what they say they would do.

People are poor at predicting what they would buy, so these questions ask about the past. Don't pitch the course until the last two minutes. Offer a $50 gift card, get consent to take notes, and don't record anyone's name in the shared notes.

Educators

  1. Tell me about the last time you used an AI tool for school work. What happened?
  2. What is your school or district telling teachers about AI right now?
  3. How did you earn your clock hours last time? How did you pick those courses, and who paid?
  4. What's the most you have paid yourself for professional development in the last two years? What was it?
  5. When during the year do you do professional development?
  6. What would make an AI course a waste of your time?
  7. Last: show the page. Would you reserve a seat today? Why or why not?

Career changers

  1. Walk me through the last month of your job search or career move.
  2. What training or courses have you paid for in the last year? How did you decide?
  3. Have AI skills come up in a job posting or interview? What did they ask?
  4. How do you show an employer you can do something new?
  5. What help have you used from WorkSource, a library or your college?
  6. What would make a credential worth $99 to you?
  7. Last: show the page. Would you reserve a seat today? Why or why not?

After each interview, write three lines in the tracking sheet: what they do now, what surprised you, and whether they reserved. Look for patterns at the weekly review.

9 · Gate rules

Agree to these before Dec 7. Each audience is judged on its own.
ResultRuleWhat happens
Pass20+ paid reservations (40+ if reservations are free), or 1+ signed letter of intent from an organizationBuild this audience's X lens in Phase 1, with the open core.
ExtendNo pass, but 150+ waitlist sign-ups with at least 40 from strangersOne 4-week follow-up: a second workshop and a direct reservation ask to the waitlist. Then pass or stop.
StopAnything elseRefund every reservation, tell the waitlist, write up what we learned.
Both pass
Build the open core and both lenses, the educator lens first because it has the School of Education partner and a renewal cycle.
One passes
Build the open core and that one lens. The other audience waits for Phase 2 data.
Only a state agency letter
Build a role-based course for that agency as a private cohort. Don't build the public lenses yet.
Nothing passes
Stop Plan B. Report back to stakeholders with the interview findings and what to try instead.

12 · Risks and safeguards

What could make the result misleading, and what we do about it
Warm-list bias
Alumni like CityU, so their numbers flatter demand. Safeguard: track strangers separately, and require 40 strangers for an "extend".
Timing
The winter break cuts two weeks from the educator test. Safeguard: front-load educator interviews before Dec 18 and hold their workshop in late January.
Overpromising
Clock hours and the credential don't exist yet. Safeguard: say "planned" or "pending" on every page, and make the refund automatic if the cohort doesn't run.
Privacy
The waitlist holds personal data. Safeguard: use it only for CityUx, keep it in a CityU system, and delete it if the audience stops and people don't opt in to hear more.
Unequal effort
If one audience gets more attention, the comparison is unfair. Safeguard: same ad budget, same number of emails, interviews and workshops for both.
Moving the goalposts
It's tempting to relax the rules after seeing the numbers. Safeguard: sign off on Section 9 before Dec 7 and report against it as written.
