Ground rule. AI will happily invent experience, numbers and skills you don't have. Everything on your resume must be true and something you can talk about in an interview. Don't paste other people's personal details into an AI tool, and if you're employed, follow your employer's rules about work documents.

Today's three moves

1 · FrameTell the AI what a new colleague would need: who it's for, the goal, the facts to use, the limits, and what it must not make up.
2 · CheckRead the result before you use it. Look for the four problems below.
3 · OwnFix it, then say in two sentences what you changed and why. You're responsible for what you send.

The four-point check

Facts. Did it change, drop or invent anything? Names, dates, numbers, rules.
Fit. Right reader, length, reading level and format?
Fairness and privacy. Is anyone left out or talked down to? Is anything shared that shouldn't be?
Voice. Does it sound like you, and like your workplace?
The example we'll watch

Honest resume bullets for a new kind of job

Weak requestWrite my resume for an operations job.
Framed requestI've been a retail shift lead for four years and I'm applying for an operations coordinator role. Below are my real duties and the job posting. Write four resume bullets for my shift-lead job that match the posting's language. Use only facts I gave you. Where a bullet would be stronger with a number I didn't give, write [add number?] instead of making one up. [my duties and the posting, from Exercise 2]

Notice the five parts in the framed request:

  1. Who it's for
  2. The goal
  3. The facts to use
  4. Limits: length, reading level, tone, format
  5. What not to make up, and what to do instead
Exercise 1 · Frame · 15 min

Frame a request: "Tell me about yourself"

Weak requestHelp me answer 'tell me about yourself' in an interview.
What you knowWho: a hiring manager for an operations coordinator job. You: four years as a retail shift lead; two years in food service before that. What you're proud of: you trained new hires, and you built a shift-schedule template the store still uses. Why this job: you like making a team's day run smoothly and want to do it full-time. Limit: 60–90 seconds spoken (about 150–200 words), plain and honest, no invented numbers.
Exercise 2 · Check · 20 min

Check the AI draft of the resume bullets

Here are the real duties and the job posting, and the bullets an AI wrote from the weak request. Compare them line by line.

Real duties, and the job posting (invented)My duties as shift lead: scheduled a team of 8; opened and closed the store; handled weekly inventory counts; trained new hires; resolved customer complaints. Posting, Operations Coordinator, Evergreen Distribution: coordinate daily schedules for a team of warehouse associates; track inventory and flag discrepancies; train new team members on procedures; resolve customer and vendor issues; prepare weekly reports in Excel. Nice to have: SQL or Tableau.
The AI draft• Managed a team of 40 associates across multiple shifts, ensuring smooth daily operations. • Reduced inventory costs by 23% through rigorous weekly inventory audits. • Trained new hires on store procedures and customer service standards. • Built weekly performance reports using advanced SQL and Tableau.

Fix it, and say what you changed

Your own task · 8 min

Try it on something from your week

Pick one task you'll do this week, or one of these. Write the framed request now; run it later with any AI assistant you're allowed to use.

  • Five likely interview questions for a job you want, with answers you check against what you've really done
  • A work sample: messy notes turned into a one-page step-by-step guide
  • A short, honest explanation of a career gap or change
  • Your LinkedIn "About" section, using only true facts

Answer key for Exercise 2 (open after the exercise)
  1. Facts: "a team of 40 associates". The duties say 8. An interviewer who asks about managing 40 people will find out quickly.
  2. Facts: "Reduced inventory costs by 23%" is a number nobody gave it. Invented results are the claims interviewers dig into most.
  3. Facts: "advanced SQL and Tableau" was copied from the posting's nice-to-haves. Claiming skills you can't show is the fastest way to lose an offer.
  4. Also worth catching: "resolved customer complaints" (a real duty that matches the posting's "resolve customer and vendor issues") was dropped, and "opened and closed the store" was lost too. The honest version is often the stronger one.
