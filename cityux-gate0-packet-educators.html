Ground rule. Never put a student's name, grades, IEP details or anything from a student record into an AI tool unless your district has approved that tool for student data. Washington's guidance for schools names FERPA, COPPA and CIPA as the laws to check against (OSPI, Human-Centered AI Guidance). Everything in this packet is invented.

Today's three moves

1 · FrameTell the AI what a new colleague would need: who it's for, the goal, the facts to use, the limits, and what it must not make up.
2 · CheckRead the result before you use it. Look for the four problems below.
3 · OwnFix it, then say in two sentences what you changed and why. You're responsible for what you send.

The four-point check

Facts. Did it change, drop or invent anything? Names, dates, numbers, rules.
Fit. Right reader, length, reading level and format?
Fairness and privacy. Is anyone left out or talked down to? Is anything shared that shouldn't be?
Voice. Does it sound like you, and like your workplace?
The example we'll watch

A note to families about a field trip

Weak requestWrite a newsletter about our science unit.
Framed requestI teach 4th grade in Washington. Many of my families read English as a second language. Write a 150-word note to families about our three-week unit on wetland ecosystems. Facts to use: field trip to the county wetland on Wed Feb 10; students need boots or old shoes; permission slips are due Wed Feb 3; the trip is free. Plain language, short sentences, no idioms, warm tone, one clear ask. Use only these facts; if something is missing, leave a [blank] instead of guessing.

Notice the five parts in the framed request:

  1. Who it's for
  2. The goal
  3. The facts to use
  4. Limits: length, reading level, tone, format
  5. What not to make up, and what to do instead

Check the AI draft of the family note

Here are the facts the teacher had, and the note an AI wrote from the weak request. Compare them line by line.

Warmly, Ms. Rivera

Reading passage for Exercise 1 (invented, grade 6)The water cycle moves water continuously between Earth's surface and the atmosphere. Energy from the sun causes water in oceans, lakes and soil to evaporate, becoming water vapor. Plants also release water vapor through their leaves, a process called transpiration. As water vapor rises, it cools and condenses into tiny droplets that form clouds. When the droplets combine and grow heavy, they fall as precipitation: rain, snow, sleet or hail. Some precipitation soaks into the ground and becomes groundwater; the rest flows over the surface as runoff into rivers, lakes and eventually the ocean, where the cycle continues.

Answer key for Exercise 2 (open after the exercise)
  1. Facts: the trip moved to "Friday, February 13". The real date is Wed Feb 10, and February 13, 2027 isn't even a Friday. A wrong date means children miss the trip.
  2. Facts and fairness: "A $15 fee covers the bus" was invented; the trip is free. A made-up fee can keep a child home.
  3. Fit and fairness: "diving into", "get the ball rolling" and "pitch in" are idioms that English learners and translation tools often get wrong.
  4. Also worth catching: "Ms. Rivera" was invented too. Check every name the AI adds.
