Hand Off a Real Task, Then Check It

A 90-minute pilot for CityU faculty and staff. Thank you for testing this with us: your feedback shapes the public workshops for K-12 educators and career changers.

  • Wed Jan 13, 2027 · noon
  • 90 minutes, online
  • Internal pilot
Ground rule. Don't put student names, grades, ID numbers or anything from a student record into an AI tool unless CityU has approved that tool for student data. Everything in this packet is invented.

Today's three moves

1 · FrameTell the AI what a new colleague would need: who it's for, the goal, the facts to use, the limits, and what it must not make up.
2 · CheckRead the result before you use it. Look for the four problems below.
3 · OwnFix it, then say in two sentences what you changed and why. You're responsible for what you send.

The four-point check

Facts. Did it change, drop or invent anything? Names, dates, numbers, rules.
Fit. Right reader, length, reading level and format?
Fairness and privacy. Is anyone left out or talked down to? Is anything shared that shouldn't be?
Voice. Does it sound like you, and like your workplace?
The example we'll watch

Turning a policy into a note students will read

Weak requestMake this policy easier for students to read.
Framed requestI work at a university with many adult students who study while working. Rewrite the late-work policy below as a short note to students (under 120 words, plain language, about an 8th-grade reading level, friendly but clear). Keep every rule and number exactly as written, including the exception. Don't add rules. End with who to contact. [the policy in Exercise 2]

Notice the five parts in the framed request:

  1. Who it's for
  2. The goal
  3. The facts to use
  4. Limits: length, reading level, tone, format
  5. What not to make up, and what to do instead
Exercise 1 · Frame · 15 min

Frame a request: an advising reminder

Weak requestWrite an email reminding students about advising.
What you knowWho: adult undergraduates, most working full-time, many studying online. Spring registration opens Mon Feb 1. Students must meet an advisor before they can register. Meetings are 30 minutes, online or on campus. Book through the advising page. Evening appointments are available Tuesdays and Thursdays.
Exercise 2 · Check · 20 min

Check the AI draft of the late-work note

Here is the original policy, and the note an AI wrote from the weak request. Compare them line by line.

The original policy (invented)Late Work Policy. Assignments submitted after the posted due date are accepted for up to 72 hours, with a 10% deduction from the earned score. Work submitted more than 72 hours late is not accepted, except when a student has a documented emergency; in that case the instructor will set a new due date. Students should contact their instructor before the due date whenever possible. Questions about this policy may be directed to the instructor or to Student Services.
The AI draftHey team! 🎉 Life happens, and we get it! If you miss a deadline, you can still turn in your work for up to three school days. There's a small 10% deduction, but after that the door closes, so plan ahead! Got questions? Reach out to your instructor anytime.

There are at least three problems. Write each one down and name which of the four checks it fails.

Exercise 3 · Own · 15 min

Fix it, and say what you changed

Rewrite the AI draft from Exercise 2 so you'd be happy to send it. Then tell your group, in two sentences, what you changed and why.

Your own task · 8 min

Try it on something from your week

Pick one task you'll do this week, or one of these. Write the framed request now; run it later with any AI assistant you're allowed to use.

  • A course announcement for next week
  • A rubric criterion rewritten in plain language for students
  • A short summary of meeting notes (remove names and personal details first)
  • A step-by-step FAQ for a process students often get wrong

Tell us what to fix

Please fill in the feedback form linked in the chat before you leave. We want to know which part was most useful, where you got lost or felt rushed, whether the 3D scene helped, and what to change before we run this for K-12 teachers (Jan 20) and career changers (Jan 26).

If you know a teacher or someone changing careers who would like a free workshop, please pass it on.

Answer key for Exercise 2 (open after the exercise)
  1. Facts: "up to three school days" is not "72 hours". Three school days can stretch over a weekend into five calendar days, so students get a different deadline than the policy allows.
  2. Fairness: the documented-emergency exception is gone. The students who most need it would never learn it exists.
  3. Voice and fit: "Hey team! 🎉" and "the door closes" don't suit a policy note, and the jokey tone can read as not taking the rule seriously.
  4. Also worth catching: Student Services was dropped as a contact, and "contact your instructor before the due date" became "anytime".
