2 · What every workshop teaches

Module 1 of the free path: hand off a real task, then check it

The free path points learners to existing free AI courses for the basics. What CityUx adds is practice on real work. Module 1 is the smallest useful piece of that: three moves people can use the next morning.

1 · Frame

Tell the AI what a new colleague would need: who it's for, the goal, the facts it must use, limits (length, reading level, tone), and what it must not make up.

2 · Check

Read the result against a four-point check before using it: facts (anything changed or invented?), fit (right reader, length, level?), fairness and privacy (anyone left out, anything shared that shouldn't be?), voice (does it sound like you?).

3 · Own

Fix it, then say in two sentences what you changed and why. You are responsible for anything you send. This is also a small version of the live defense in the planned $99 credential.

By the end, participants can

  • Rewrite a weak one-line request into a framed request for a task from their own job.
  • Find at least two planted problems in an AI draft using the four-point check.
  • Explain, in their own words, what they changed in a draft and why.

For educators, this matches the order in Washington's guidance for schools, which asks that AI use start with human inquiry and end with human reflection ("Human, AI, Human") OSPI. Say so in the educator workshop; it shows the lesson fits what their state already expects.

4 · Workshop 1: CityU faculty and staff pilot

Wed Jan 13, noon · a dress rehearsal that also helps colleagues

Purpose. Test the material, the timing, the breakouts and the 3D scene before the public sessions, and collect feedback. Invite 15–30 colleagues, including a few from the School of Education and Student Services. Its numbers are kept out of the gate count.

Example: weak and framed requests

WeakMake this policy easier for students to read.
FramedI work at a university with many adult students who study while working. Rewrite the late-work policy below as a short note to students (under 120 words, plain language, about an 8th-grade reading level, friendly but clear). Keep every rule and number exactly as written, including the exception. Don't add rules. End with who to contact. [policy paragraph from the packet]

Check exercise: the planted problems

The packet's policy says work is accepted up to 72 hours late with a 10% deduction, and that documented emergencies are handled by the instructor. The AI draft in the packet:

  • changes "72 hours" to "three school days" (a fact change);
  • drops the emergency exception entirely (fairness: the students who most need it lose it);
  • opens with "Hey team! 🎉", which doesn't fit a policy note (voice and fit).

Feedback form (replaces the offer)

  • Which segment was most useful? Which would you cut?
  • Where did you get lost or feel rushed?
  • Did the 3D scene help you think, or get in the way?
  • Would you send a colleague, a student or a family member to the public version? Who?
  • Anything you'd change before we show this to K-12 teachers or job seekers?

5 · Workshop 2: K-12 educators

Wed Jan 20, 4:00 pm · co-led with the School of Education partner

Title on the sign-up page: Use AI Well in the Work You Already Do: A Free 90-Minute Workshop for Washington Educators. After school hours, so teachers can join from their classroom.

Ground rule, said out loud at minute 2: never put a student's name, grades, IEP details or anything from a student record into an AI tool unless your district has approved that tool for student data. Washington's guidance names FERPA, COPPA and CIPA as the laws to check against OSPI. Everything in today's packet is invented.

Example: weak and framed requests

WeakWrite a newsletter about our science unit.
FramedI teach 4th grade in Washington. Many of my families read English as a second language. Write a 150-word note to families about our three-week unit on wetland ecosystems. Facts to use: field trip to the county wetland on Wed Feb 10; students need boots or old shoes; permission slips are due Wed Feb 3; the trip is free. Plain language, short sentences, no idioms, warm tone, one clear ask. Use only these facts; if something is missing, leave a [blank] instead of guessing.

Check exercise: the planted problems

  • The trip date becomes "Friday, February 13" (fact).
  • It adds "a $15 fee covers the bus" when the trip is free (fact, and a real barrier for families).
  • It asks families to "pitch in and get the ball rolling", two idioms that are hard for English learners and for translation tools (fit and fairness).

Packet tasks for "your own task"

  • Rewrite a reading passage at three levels for the same lesson.
  • Draft three feedback comments on a short invented student paragraph, then check them against your own rubric.
  • Turn a dense district announcement into a family-friendly note.

Link the OSPI classroom guide's five-level AI Scaffolding Scale in the follow-up email for teachers who want to set rules for student use OSPI. Mention clock hours only as "planned, pending approval".

6 · Workshop 3: Career changers

Tue Jan 26, 6:00 pm · evening, for people who are working

Title on the sign-up page: Show Employers You Can Use AI on Real Work: A Free 90-Minute Workshop from City University of Seattle. The examples come from moves people make often: retail or hospitality to operations, admin to project coordination, and returning to work after time away.

Ground rule, said out loud at minute 2: AI will happily invent experience, numbers and skills you don't have. Anything on your resume has to be true and something you can talk about in an interview. Don't paste in other people's personal details.

Example: weak and framed requests

WeakWrite my resume for an operations job.
FramedI've been a retail shift lead for four years and I'm applying for an operations coordinator role. Below are my real duties and the job posting. Write four resume bullets for my shift-lead job that match the posting's language. Use only facts I gave you. Where a bullet would be stronger with a number I didn't give, write [add number?] instead of making one up. [duties: scheduled a team of 8; opened and closed the store; handled weekly inventory counts; trained new hires; resolved customer complaints] [posting excerpt from the packet]

Check exercise: the planted problems

  • "Managed a team of 40 associates" when the duties say 8 (fact).
  • "Reduced inventory costs by 23%", a number nobody gave it (fact, and the kind interviewers ask about).
  • "Advanced SQL and Tableau reporting" added to match the posting (fact; claiming skills you can't show is the fastest way to lose an offer).

Packet tasks for "your own task"

  • Prepare for an interview: ask AI for five likely questions for the posting, then check its answers against what you've really done.
  • Make a work sample: turn a messy set of notes into a one-page step-by-step guide, the kind of thing a coordinator writes.
  • Write a short, honest explanation of a career gap or change.

Point people to free help that already exists in the area, such as WorkSource and public library job-help programs, in the follow-up email. CityUx is the next step after those, not a replacement.

7 · The 10-minute offer

Honest, short, and easy to say no to

Draft script for the host, minutes 80–85. Adjust the bracketed parts for each audience.

Everything you did today is free, and the free learning path stays free. We're deciding now whether to build the next part: a coached cohort this spring, four live weeks with feedback on your own work[, and a $99 credential: a real task from your field, scored by a person, with credit toward a CityU program planned]. We'll only build it if enough people want it. So we're asking for a $25 reservation. It counts toward the cohort price, you can get it back any time before the cohort starts, and if we don't run the cohort, you get it back automatically. [If you're out of work right now, check the box and reserve for free.] [If you'd like this for the teachers in your building, there's a separate box: bring a cohort to your school.] If you're not ready, joining the waitlist is free and still helps us. The link is in the chat.

Minutes 85–90: exit poll, the same three questions in every external workshop: How useful was today (1–5)? Would you pay $99 for a CityU credential that proves you can use AI at work (yes, maybe, no)? What's one task you'll try this week?

Don't say: that clock hours, credit or the credential are approved; that the cohort "is filling up"; or anything that pressures people. Gate 0 needs real interest, and pressure would inflate it.

The links in the chat carry the workshop's channel tag, so reservations within 72 hours count as workshop conversions, as Gate 0 Section 4 sets out.

8 · People, tools and materials

Three people per session, free tools, everything shared ahead
Host
Teaches. STC faculty for all three; the educator session is co-led with the School of Education partner.
Producer
Runs polls, breakouts, chat links and the 3D scene; watches the chat for people who are stuck.
Note-taker
Logs questions, objections and quotes for the gate memo. No names in the shared notes.
Platform
CityU's video platform, with live captions on and the main room recorded; breakouts never recorded.
AI tools
Whatever free AI assistant people already have, or their district- or employer-approved tool. The host demos on one free tier. No one needs an account: the practice works on paper or in the chat.
Packet
A short PDF and web page: the weak requests, the AI drafts with planted problems, the four-point check, the answer key and the own-task prompts. Licensed CC BY 4.0. Sent the day before. Drafts: staff pilot · K-12 educators · career changers. Each prints cleanly from the browser (the answer key is left out of the printout).
Accessibility
Slides and packet shared ahead in accessible formats; nothing depends on color alone; the 3D scene has a text description.
Follow-up
Within 24 hours: recording, packet, free-path link, the reservation and waitlist links, and the audience-specific links above.
