CityUx Labs: AI for Life-Science Professionals

A free, browser-only lab site that turns the "AI for Life-Science Professionals" idea from the Nov 11 life sciences briefing into something people can click. It's a CityUx prototype, so it doesn't follow STC course standards, but it keeps the CityU brand, accessibility, and the free-and-public-data rules.

The four labs

Each about 15–20 minutes, no install, no account, no API key
Lab 1
Protein Structure Explorer. Puts a lab-measured structure from the Protein Data Bank next to AlphaFold's prediction, colored by pLDDT confidence. Five proteins (lysozyme, GFP, hemoglobin, insulin, p53), fetched live. Teaches that low confidence often marks flexible biology or parts that get cut away, not just AI error.
Lab 2
Assay Hit Predictor. A k-nearest-neighbors model on simulated screening data. Learners change the amount of data, the assay noise, and the model's flexibility, and compare training accuracy, test accuracy, and an "always inactive" baseline.
Lab 3
Literature Triage Agent. A rule-based agent screens ten fictional abstracts (a made-up drug, Velorastat) against review criteria. Learners check its work, then switch on upgrades and watch precision and recall change. The traps are negation, code names, reviews, retractions, animal studies, and computer-only studies.
Lab 4
Can This Data Cross the Border? Eight Seattle–Shenzhen data-sharing scenarios, each answered with share, review first, or don't share. Covers China's human genetic resources rules, the US bulk sensitive data rules, restricted companies, FERPA/PIPL, and public or synthetic data. It's the "methods, not data" guardrail from the briefing, made hands-on.

Where it fits

The CityUx funnel

The labs sit at the GitHub / self-paced stage of the CityUx open-ecosystem funnel. Next steps along the funnel could be:

  • Embed the labs in an Open edX micro-credential ("AI for Life-Science Professionals").
  • Use them in live workshops for employers and displaced biotech workers.
  • Use Lab 4, and possibly Lab 1, in a joint seminar with Shenzhen Polytechnic University, since it uses only public data.

Open items

Before this goes beyond a prototype
  • 1
    Domain review. No life-science expert has checked Labs 1–3. Possible reviewers include Shenzhen Poly's School of Food and Drug or CityU's health side.
  • 2
    Compliance review of Lab 4. It simplifies legal rules for teaching. A compliance or export-control office should read it before wider use.
  • 3
    Home. Decide whether and when it moves from Clark's GitHub to the cityuseattle org.
  • 4
    Live data dependency. Lab 1 fetches from RCSB and the AlphaFold Database. If either changes its URLs, the lab shows an error message rather than breaking silently; check it before any workshop.
