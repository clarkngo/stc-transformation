Plan B Addendum: Three More Angles to Test

Three ways to make Plan B's paid layer stronger without changing its model (free learning, paid proof, people and private cohorts): train district trainers instead of single teachers, get career changers' seats paid by workforce funds, and teach in other languages through DeoSpheres' community partners. Each one is tested inside Gate 0 and decided at the Feb 17 gate.

1 · The addendum in one page

Same model, better payers

Plan B's weakest point is that individual learners rarely pay. These three angles each move the bill to someone who pays more reliably: a district or service district, a workforce fund, or a grant and a community partner. None of them needs anything built before Feb 17. Angles 1 and 2 add only interview questions and partner calls to Gate 0; Angle 3 adds one workshop.

2 · Angle 1: Train the trainers for educators

Sell capacity to districts, not seats to teachers

The idea. Instead of CityU teaching every cohort of teachers, CityU certifies district professional-development leads and service-district coaches to run the educator lens themselves. CityU sells three things: a facilitator kit (the sessions, packets, examples and rubrics, kept CC BY so the content stays open), facilitator certification (a short coached course plus a scored practice session), and the human-scored credential for teachers who want it, scored centrally by CityU.

Why
The competitor scan found teacher clock hours are cheap and close by: service districts sell short AI courses for about $3 a clock hour ESD 113 on pdEnroller, and WEA grants 15 clock hours per NEA micro-credential WEA. A $99–$149 seat competes with that price. A district buying the ability to train all its own teachers is a different, larger purchase, and service districts become partners rather than competitors.
Who else
Anthropic offers a free train-the-trainer course through AFT, and Microsoft's Elevate for Educators is free with live sessions Microsoft. CityUx's case: not tied to one AI company's tools, built around Washington's own "Human, AI, Human" guidance OSPI, co-owned by CityU's School of Education, with human scoring and a credit path.
Prices to test
Facilitator certification per person, and a district kit license sized by number of teachers. These are hypotheses for the interviews, not quotes: ask what the district paid for its last outside PD package before naming a number.
Watch out
Quality drifts when others teach. Keep the credential scored centrally, certify facilitators with a scored practice run, and refresh the kit each term. It can also undercut the district-cohort price in Plan B, so treat the two as one offer with two delivery options.

3 · Angle 2: Funded seats for career changers

Turn "free if you're out of work" from a cost into income

The idea. Plan B offers the coached cohort free or reduced to unemployed learners. If the cohort and credential qualify for public workforce funding, those seats are paid for instead. Plan B already puts this in Phase 3 (2028); this angle starts the eligibility work now so the first cohorts collect the outcome data the funders require.

RouteWhat it paysFit for Plan B
WIOA training accounts, through the Eligible Training Provider List on Career Bridge Career BridgeTuition for eligible job seekers, through WorkSourceThe nearest route. Unknown: whether a private nonprofit university's non-credit program qualifies. Ask now.
ESD Training Benefits ESDExtends unemployment benefits during approved full-time trainingOnly for claimants whose past jobs paid under $22.26 an hour on average (2026). Fits service workers, not most laid-off tech workers, and a part-time 4-week cohort may not count as full-time.
Workforce Pell ED fact sheetPell grants for short programs of 150–599 clock hours over 8–15 weeksNot the 4-week cohort. Possible later for a stacked program (free path + cohort + credential + more), which needs a completion and job-placement record first.
Why
Career changers are where the competitor scan found the clearest gap, but they're also the group least able to pay. A funded seat removes the biggest barrier and pays CityU the full price.
The real cost
Paperwork and performance rules: completion, employment and earnings thresholds. That makes outcome tracking from the very first cohort (completion, credential pass rate, job change at 3 and 6 months) a requirement, not an extra.

4 · Angle 3: In-language community cohorts

With DeoSpheres' Korean American and Filipino American partners

The idea. Teach the same Module 1 (Frame, Check, Own) in Korean or Tagalog, or bilingually, with examples from the community's own work, through the partners already in the DeoSpheres partnership. Bilingual facilitators come from the partners; CityU provides the lesson, the scoring and the credential.

Why
The big free AI courses in the competitor scan are almost all in English. In-language teaching is a real gap the free providers aren't filling, and it fits what community grants fund. It also gives a second route into health-care support, which the quadrant scored attractive (3.75) but hard for CityU to win alone (2.75): many Filipino American workers are in health care, and the partner brings the access CityU lacks.
Who pays
Grants, with the community partner co-applying: the DeoSpheres notes already name a Microsoft Community First grant for Korean Americans and a longer-term Microsoft Elevate partnership. Workforce boards are also partnering with Microsoft Elevate on AI skills NAWB. Individual learners stay free.
Watch out
CityU has no bilingual facilitators of its own, so the partner must supply them. Translation must be reviewed by a fluent person, not left to an AI tool, which is also a good live example of the Check move. Keep the results separate from the two main Gate 0 audiences so they don't blur either decision.
