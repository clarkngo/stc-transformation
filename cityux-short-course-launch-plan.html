Launching CityUx Short Courses: AI Fluency for X

A market study and launch plan for CityU's first paid micro-credential short courses. It covers three audiences: people who work in higher education, workers changing jobs or fields, and the skilled trades, starting with plumbing and 3D visual training.

Nov 24, 2026 Draft for discussion Horizon: Dec 2026 – Jun 2028 Prepared by: Clark Ngo, STC
See also: the critique of this plan and an alternative, Plan B.
Read this first. Market figures were checked in November 2026 against the sources listed at the end. Where sources disagree, this page says so. Prices, enrollments and costs in Sections 7 and 9 are hypotheses to test, not forecasts. Funding eligibility for L&I continuing education, the WIOA training list and Workforce Pell has to be confirmed with each agency before anything is promised to learners.

Finding
Demand for AI skills is real, but generic "AI fluency" is now free. Anthropic, OpenAI and Microsoft give away beginner AI courses with certificates, and Google's runs about $49 a month on Coursera. A general CityU "Intro to AI" would compete with free and lose.
Our lane
CityUx can win on the "for X" part. That means one specific audience, a live cohort, a real work product the learner keeps, a credential that stacks toward a CityU program, and someone other than the learner who pays: an institution's development budget, a workforce fund, a grant, an employer, or a license-renewal requirement.
Recommend
Launch three courses in two waves, one for each aim, and give each one a different payer so we aren't betting on a single funding stream:
  1. AI Fluency for Higher Education: Redesign One Thing. Wave 1, March 2027. Institutions pay for their faculty and staff. We pilot it on CityU's own people first.
  2. AI Career Pivot Lab. Wave 1, March 2027. This is for laid-off, underemployed and service-sector workers. Grants pay for the first cohorts while we build the outcome record we need for workforce funding, and later for Workforce Pell.
  3. Plumbing Systems in 3D (an L&I-approved CE course) plus AI for the Trades Shop. Wave 2, after L&I approval. Plumbers' required license-renewal hours and contractors pay.
Be honest
Year one will roughly break even at best. CityU's own micro-credential evidence review found that revenue is not the prize. The payoff is a pipeline into CityU programs, partners who come back, and an outcome record that opens bigger public funding in year two.
The ask (Section 14): approve the two-wave plan, a seed budget and staff time for three course builds, an internal CityU pilot in February 2027, and permission to start the L&I, Career Bridge and Workforce Pell eligibility conversations now, since each one takes months.

2 · Market context: why now

Three pressures meeting at once, and a fourth that changes who can pay
Higher ed
Higher education is under real strain. New international enrollment fell 18% this fall (Open Doors, via Fortune, Nov 2026). Hampshire College is one of about nine colleges set to close in 2026, and large universities have cut hundreds of positions each. Total enrollment is still flat to slightly up (fall 2025 grew 1% to 19.4M). The crisis shows up as budget pressure, cuts and the question of what a degree is for. Faculty and staff are using AI already but mostly on their own: only 37% of institutions run upskilling, and 80% of those mostly expect people to learn by themselves (EDUCAUSE 2025).
Jobs
The labor market is growing without much hiring, and the pain is concentrated. In October 2026 the U.S. added 165,000 jobs and unemployment held at 4.1%. Washington is worse. It ran 5.2% in June and 4.8% in September, Seattle has matched or exceeded the state rate every month since September 2025, and the state's tech layoffs are the second highest in the country. Recent college graduates are unemployed at about 5.6%, above the overall rate, and about 42.5% are underemployed (NY Fed). Employers cited AI in roughly 121,000 announced U.S. job cuts from January through October 2026, about 20% of all cuts (Challenger).
Growth
The growth is in care, service and hands-on work. Health care and social assistance added 680,500 jobs in the year to March 2026, by far the largest gain. Without that sector, the rest of the private sector averaged only about 35,000 jobs a month in 2026 (Deloitte). Public two-year vocational enrollment has grown for several years running, as students look for careers less exposed to AI. The plumbing trade alone has about 44,000 openings a year nationally, at a median wage of $62,970 (BLS/O*NET).
AI premium
AI skills pay, and mostly outside tech. Job postings that ask for at least one AI skill advertise about 28% higher pay, and about 51% of those postings are outside IT and computer science (Lightcast, 2024 data). The learners who gain most are not engineers. They are people in every other job who learn to use AI well.
New money
Public money for short programs opened in 2026. Since July 1, 2026, Workforce Pell covers programs of 150–599 clock hours that run 8 to 15 weeks. Programs need governor approval, a 70% completion benchmark and a track record. Washington's existing routes (WIOA training accounts through the Eligible Training Provider List, and ESD Training Benefits) also pay for or support approved training. Each has performance rules, so outcome data from the first cohorts is a strategic asset, not paperwork.

3 · Demand evidence for the three aims

What the data says about each audience, and what it means for course design

Aim A: people who work in higher education

SignalEvidenceWhat it means for us
Use is near universal, guidance is not94% of higher-ed employees used AI for work in the last six months; 54% know their institution's AI policy (EDUCAUSE 2026)Sell safe, policy-aware practice, not awareness. Data privacy, FERPA and accessibility have to be built in.
Training is the top planning item"Faculty training" is the most common element in institutions' AI strategic plans; AI literacy is EDUCAUSE's #2 issue for 2026A budget line exists. The buyer is the provost's office, the teaching center or HR.
Training is mostly do-it-yourselfOf institutions that upskill, 80% encourage self-directed learning and 71% offer in-house sessionsMany teaching centers are stretched thin. A ready-made cohort course they can license fills a real gap.
People want control over AI in assessmentFaculty and staff want to choose when to use AI tools for assessment (EDUCAUSE, June 2026)Teach a decision method (which activity, which rule), the way STC's AI 410 AI Use Guide does.
Jobs inside higher ed are at riskClosures and cuts in 2026 (Section 2)Some staff need a career pivot, not just a workflow upgrade. Point them to the Career Pivot Lab.

Aim B: the trades, starting with plumbing and 3D visual training

SignalEvidenceWhat it means for us
Training is required by lawWA journey-level and residential plumbers need 24 approved CE hours per 3-year renewal, at least 12 of them in plumbing. Trainees need 8 hours a year. L&I approves courses and providers report rosters within 30 days.Built-in, recurring demand. The 3D course has to be plumbing content (code, systems, troubleshooting) to count. AI-for-business material goes in a separate course.
The work is growing and pays wellAbout 504,500 U.S. plumbers, pipefitters and steamfitters; about 44,000 openings a year; median pay $62,970 (BLS 2024)A steady pool of trainees and career changers moving into the trade.
Contractors use AI but don't know how to get value from it69% of plumbing contractors use AI, and productivity gains outpace profit gains (ServiceTitan, Sept 2026). In an April residential survey, more than half weren't sure where to start.Shop owners and office managers need practical help with estimating, scheduling, customer messages and safe code lookup.
3D and VR training is catching on, but the evidence is thinInterplay Learning's 3D/VR simulations are now built into HVAC and plumbing apprenticeships (Modigent). Peer-reviewed proof of better job skills is still scarce, and one 2025 plumbing AR study measured confidence only.Don't promise outcomes. Measure them with a pre and post scenario test, and say on day one that 3D supports hands-on work rather than replacing it.
Young people are choosing tradesPublic two-year vocational enrollment rose about 20% from 2020 to 2025 and kept growing in 2026. Quartz cautions that the fastest growth is in culinary and health programs, and that some viral numbers are overstated.Speak to the "AI-proof career" motive honestly. The trades are using AI too, so being fluent in it is an advantage.

Aim C: helping people change jobs or fields

SignalEvidenceWhat it means for us
Entry-level hiring has shrunkWorkers aged 22–25 in the most AI-exposed jobs are 19% below where they would be if they had kept pace with less-exposed peers. The cause is less hiring, not more firing (Stanford Digital Economy Lab, Aug 2026).Recent grads need proof of work, not another certificate. Every Pivot Lab learner builds work samples.
Seattle tech workers were hit hardWashington tech lost more than 11,000 workers (May 2025 – April 2026, Revelio); Seattle metro unemployment rose above 5%A local audience that is skilled, motivated and often has severance. Employers running layoffs can also buy the course as an outplacement benefit.
People are moving into service and care workHealth care drives job growth; leisure and hospitality is a distant secondPivot Lab tracks point at where hiring actually is: health-care operations and administration, customer and AI operations, and running a small business.
Retraining support comes with fine printESD Training Benefits extends unemployment benefits for approved, full-time training, but only for claimants whose past jobs paid under $22.26 an hour on average (2026)That route fits service workers, not most laid-off tech workers. Plan a separate payer for each group (Section 7).

The trades courses score lower on their own but go together: one is required by law, the other gets contractors through the door. Health care is the largest growth sector, but it moves to Wave 3 because hospital training buyers take longer and the health-care partner channel still has to be built.

One core, many "X" versions. All AI Fluency for X courses share a 2-module core (what AI is and isn't, plus safe everyday use with checking and privacy), built once on Open edX. Each audience adds 2–4 modules of its own and a final work product. That keeps the cost of each new audience low, which is how "for X" can grow to more audiences without starting over each time.

6 · The three launch courses

Each is cohort-based, example-first, ends in something the learner keeps, and earns a CityUx digital credential
Wave 1 · Mar 2027

A · AI Fluency for Higher Education: Redesign One Thing

"In four weeks, redesign one assignment, one process or one policy in your own job, and be able to defend it."

WhoFaculty, academic and student-services staff, and academic leaders. Three tracks share one cohort: Teach, Serve and Lead.
Format4 weeks, about 16 hours: 3 self-paced hours plus a 1-hour live session each week. Cohorts of 20–25.
Modules1) Shared core: how AI works, hands-on first. 2) Shared core: guardrails (FERPA, privacy, accessibility, bias, checking output). 3) Track sprint: Teach redesigns an assessment with per-activity AI rules; Serve redesigns an advising or admissions workflow; Lead drafts an AI policy and staff-training plan. 4) Show and defend to peers, with a rubric.
Learner keepsA redesigned artifact ready to use, plus a one-page rationale
CredentialCityUx digital badge. Explore whether CityU's School of Education could accept it toward its programs.
Price (test)$395 per person; institutional cohort license of $6,000 for up to 25 seats (about $240 each); free internal pilot for CityU staff
ChannelsCityU's own staff first, then independent colleges in Washington and the Northwest, community and technical colleges (alongside Microsoft Elevate rather than against it), EDUCAUSE and regional conference talks
Leads toCityU School of Education programs, and STC certificates for staff moving into tech roles
Wave 1 · Mar 2027

B · AI Career Pivot Lab

"Six weeks from 'my job is shrinking' to a target role, three work samples, and a plan you can act on."

WhoLaid-off and underemployed workers in the Seattle area: tech-adjacent staff, recent grads, higher-ed staff, and service workers who want to move up
Format6 weeks, about 36 hours: two evening live sessions a week plus lab time. Cohorts of 20. Built as modules that can later stack into a 150+ clock-hour program.
Modules1) Skills inventory and a map of Washington's labor market, using state and federal data. 2) AI fluency core. 3–4) A domain sprint in one track (health-care operations and administration, customer and AI operations, or small-business operations) where learners build two work samples with AI. 5) An honest AI-assisted job search: tailoring, practice interviews, no made-up experience. 6) Demo day with employers.
Learner keepsA portfolio with three work samples, a target-role plan, and an employer contact list
CredentialCityUx badge. In year two, stack it into an "Applied AI Workforce Certificate" of 150+ clock hours over 8–14 weeks, sized for Workforce Pell and the training provider list.
Price (test)$495 list. The goal is $0 to the learner, paid by a grant, workforce funds or an employer's outplacement budget.
ChannelsWorkSource and the King and Snohomish workforce boards, outplacement firms, community partners, alumni networks, LinkedIn
Leads toSTC's CS 11A/11B bridge courses into the graduate programs, STC bachelor's programs and graduate certificates
Wave 2 · from Jun 2027

C · Plumbing Systems in 3D + AI for the Trades Shop

"See inside the wall before you open it, and run the shop with less paperwork."

C1 · 3D courseAn 8-hour L&I CE course (pending approval). An interactive browser 3D model of drain-waste-vent and water-supply systems. Learners work through code scenarios (venting, slope, fixture units, backflow) and diagnose faults in 3D, then check their answer against the code text. It runs on a phone or tablet in the field. Also usable by trainees for their 8 hours a year.
C2 · AI for the Shop4 evenings, about 8 hours, for owners and office managers: estimating and quoting, scheduling and dispatch, customer messages, and code or manual lookup with verification (AI can be wrong about code). Not CE-eligible; sold to employers.
Learner keepsC1: CE hours reported to L&I. C2: the shop's own set of prompts and templates, plus an AI-use checklist.
Price (test)C1 $129 per learner (benchmark against current WA CE prices before launch). C2 $295 per seat, or $1,500 for a shop's team.
PartnersA union training center or a community or technical college with hands-on labs (for credibility and reach)
BuildBrowser 3D (three.js) on the same free, static, no-API-key pattern as STC's other labs. A licensed plumber reviews all code content.
Leads toRepeat CE purchases each renewal cycle, the AI Fluency for X core, and STC programs for workers' families and office staff

10 · Launch roadmap

Eighteen months, with a go / fix / stop review after Wave 1
  1. Dec 2026
    Decide and start the long-lead tasks. Stakeholders approve the plan; form a steering group; contact L&I (CE provider and course approval), Career Bridge (training-list eligibility) and the state Workforce Board (Workforce Pell process); line up 2 pilot institutions for Course A and 1 trades training partner.
  2. Jan – Feb 2027
    Build Wave 1. Build the shared AI Fluency core plus Courses A and B on Open edX (WCAG 2.2 AA, free tools only). Run Course A free for 20 CityU faculty and staff in February. Recruit the first Pivot Lab cohort through WorkSource and partners. Submit the 3D course to L&I for approval.
  3. Mar 2027
    Public launch of Wave 1. First paid Course A cohort (with pilot institutions) and Pivot Lab cohort 1 (grant-funded, founding pricing).
  4. Apr – Jun 2027
    Build and pilot Wave 2. Build the 3D plumbing model and the AI for the Shop course; licensed-plumber review; pilot with the training partner, with pre and post scenario tests. Pivot Lab cohort 2. Collect 3-month outcome data.
  5. Jul 2027
    Review gate. Go, fix or stop for each course against the Section 11 targets. Report to stakeholders.
  6. Aug – Dec 2027
    Scale what works. Course A institutional licenses beyond the Northwest; Wave 3 (health-care support workers, life sciences); apply to the training provider list with Pivot Lab outcome data; design the 150+ hour stackable certificate.
  7. Jan – Jun 2028
    Bigger funding. With a year of outcomes, apply for Workforce Pell through the governor's process and pursue a Microsoft Elevate partnership. Decide whether to make CityUx short courses a permanent line.
