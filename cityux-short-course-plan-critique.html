Plan Review — CityUx

Short-Course Launch: Critique of Plan A, and Plan B

A frank review of the three-course launch plan (Plan A), followed by a different approach, Plan B: an open, free AI Fluency for X course where CityUx charges for assessed proof and live coaching instead of content, with demand tested before anything big is built.

Updated Nov 24: Plan B's test audiences now come from the target-market quadrant: K-12 educators, career changers, and state agencies (partner exploration). The trades are parked. Sections 4–7 and 10 reflect this.

1 · Verdict on Plan A

Good diagnosis, not ready to present as a launch plan

Plan A reads the market correctly: generic AI fluency is free, so CityUx has to win on specificity and on who pays. But it acts as if those questions are settled. It launches three products into three unrelated markets in about ten weeks, with a small team that is also launching the BSAI, payers that are mostly unconfirmed, and no conversations yet with a single real customer. Stakeholders who read it closely will ask, "How do you know anyone will buy this?" Plan A has no answer yet.

2 · What Plan A gets right (Plan B keeps all of it)

  • No generic "Intro to AI." Competing with free content is a losing game.
  • "Who pays?" is asked for every course. The learner's own wallet is the weakest payer.
  • One shared core with an "X" layer for each audience, which keeps the cost of each new audience low.
  • Outcome data treated as an asset, since the state training list and Workforce Pell both require it.
  • A go / fix / stop review with targets set in advance.
  • Cohorts and a work product the learner keeps, rather than videos alone.
  • Honest about revenue. It matches CityU's own micro-credential evidence review.
⚠️

3 · Eleven problems with Plan A

Most serious first. Each has a fix, and Plan B is built from the fixes.
High

1. No primary research: we haven't talked to a buyer

Every demand claim comes from national reports. Nobody has interviewed a teaching-center director, a laid-off Seattle worker or a plumbing shop owner, and nobody has tested a price or collected a waitlist. The course-scoring table gives "demand" scores that are really our own guesses, and it rates CityU's own strengths highly, which is the score most open to bias.

Fix: run a short validation sprint (interviews, landing pages, refundable seat reservations, free live workshops) before building any course.

High

2. Three markets at once is three startups

Higher-ed buyers, workforce agencies and unemployed adults, and trades contractors share almost nothing: different buyers, different sales cycles, different partners, different expertise. Plan A calls having three payers "diversification," but it actually triples the marketing and sales work. And the team is the same three STC people currently rebuilding AI 410–440 for the BSAI's winter launch.

Fix: pick one beachhead audience and one product. Treat the other aims as experiments that have to earn their way in.

High

3. The timeline doesn't hold up

Approval in December and paid external cohorts in March leaves about ten weeks, including the holidays, to build two courses, run an internal pilot, sign outside institutions and recruit an unemployed cohort. Institutions usually buy staff training in their annual budget cycle, not within weeks. The Career Pivot cohort is "grant-funded," but no grant has been secured.

Fix: make March a free pilot and evidence milestone, not a paid launch, and set paid dates by what the pilot shows.

High

4. Most of the payers are hopes

Of the eight payers in Plan A, only institutional budgets, employer outplacement and self-paying learners are available now. Institutions are in a budget crisis by Plan A's own account, and CityU's evidence review found only about 20% of learners interested in a paid micro-credential. Training-list eligibility for a private university's non-credit program is unknown. ESD Training Benefits excludes most laid-off tech workers. Workforce Pell is 2028 at the earliest. L&I approval hasn't been requested.

Fix: a model that works with low paid conversion, where free reach builds the case for grant funders, and the agency eligibility work runs alongside rather than blocking the launch.

Medium

5. The higher-ed course sells into shrinking budgets and to competitors

The same section that describes college budget cuts expects colleges to buy $6,000 licenses. Other universities may not want staff training from a peer institution they compete with for students. Free AI courses made specifically for educators already exist from large tech companies, and teaching-center associations also sell faculty development.

Fix: start inside CityU, where CityU is both the buyer and the proof. Sell outside only once there are results to show, and lead with "open, free to adapt, with paid facilitation."

Medium

6. The trades market is small and crowded, and CityU has no standing there

Washington employs about 12,000 plumbers (BLS via a secondary source).Plumber count Each needs about 8 CE hours a yearL&I, and L&I's own Contractor Training Days, union training centers and supply houses already provide them, often cheaply. CityU has no licensed plumbers on staff. A credible 3D plumbing model costs far more than the $15,000 per course Plan A budgets. And by keeping AI out of the CE course to qualify for credit, Plan A split your 3D idea away from the AI Fluency brand.

Fix: park the trades. Take the same required-renewal idea to K-12 educators, who need 100 clock hours per five-year renewal and already trust CityU's School of Education (see the quadrant). Keep 3D as a teaching approach across all courses.

Medium

7. The Career Pivot Lab promises outcomes we can't control

"50% in a new job within six months" and an employer demo day both depend on employers who aren't lined up, in a market where entry-level hiring is shrinkingStanford. Charging unemployed people for a course whose results are uncertain is also an ethical and reputational risk. And if the outcomes come in poor, the data that was meant to open public funding will instead rule CityU out.

Fix: promise what CityUx controls (a portfolio, an assessed credential, coaching). Report job outcomes, but don't sell on them until we have them. Keep it free or nearly free for unemployed learners.

Medium

8. The money model is optimistic

The base case needs 320 paying learners in year one in markets where CityUx has no name yet. It leaves out sales and partnership time, learner support, a badge platform, refunds and overhead, and it counts a $20,000 grant that hasn't been won. A base-case profit of $10,000 is within the margin of error of zero, and the conservative case loses $21,660.

Fix: spend less up front, and add the missing costs to the model.

Medium

9. "It feeds CityU degrees" is assumed, not shown

The main justification is that learners move on to CityU programs, but the evidence review says the step from micro-credential to degree is weak in the research. Plan A measures program inquiries but has no specific design for turning learners into students.

Fix: build in specific next steps, such as credit for the assessed credential, a named advisor, and a tuition credit for completers. Track the share of learners who actually enroll.

Lower

10. It drifts from the CityUx strategy already approved

The president approved an open funnel: free YouTube and GitHub content and self-paced Open edX courses at the top and middle, with paid and high-touch offerings at the bottom. The board briefing set out an open-source content model. Plan A goes to paid courses first, with small free samples.

Fix: keep the content open, as the approved funnel does, and charge at the bottom of the funnel.

Lower

11. No market sizing

There is no count of how many people in each audience CityUx could realistically reach (for example, higher-ed employees in Washington, displaced workers in King and Snohomish counties, or certified plumbers). Stakeholders will ask for these numbers.

Fix: started in the quadrant, which sizes 12 Washington audiences. Phase 0 fills the gaps, such as the exact 2025–26 teacher count from OSPI's personnel report.

4 · Plan B: Open Core, Prove Then Scale

Give the learning away. Charge for proof, people and private cohorts.

Free providers have made AI content worthless as a product. What they don't offer is a human-assessed credential from an accredited university, live coaching, and versions built for specific jobs. Plan B gives away an open AI Fluency for X course and charges for those three things. It also follows the CityUx funnel the president approved, and it starts with the people CityU already knows.

Paid · organizations
Private cohortsFor employers, colleges and partners: their own cohort, their examples, completion reports. $3K–$8K per cohort.
Paid · individuals
Proof and peopleProof of AI Fluency, a human-scored performance task plus a short live defense, $99. Coached cohort, 4 live weeks with feedback, $249. Free or discounted for unemployed learners.
Free · open (CC BY)
AI Fluency for X: open core4 self-paced modules on Open edX and YouTube, taught example-first, plus short "X lenses" for specific jobs, starting with K-12 educators and career changers. Anyone can use it, including other colleges.
Beachhead
Two audiences where CityU is already trusted: (1) K-12 educators, who need 100 clock hours per five-year renewal and whom CityU's School of Education already prepares; and (2) career changers, starting with CityU alumni, then office and admin staff, workers over 50 and recent grads through WorkSource. CityU's own faculty and staff are the internal pilot. State agencies, whose staff must all take AI training under state policy, are explored as a partner play. See the quadrant for why.
Credential
The paid credential reuses STC's spec, build and defend assessment model and the CityUx assessment playbook: a real task from the learner's own job, a rubric, and a 10-minute defense like the AI 410 faculty interview. That is something free providers don't offer, and the evidence review found employers trust assessed, university-issued credentials most.
Funding
Grant funders and AI skilling programs (for example Microsoft Elevate, philanthropy and workforce boards) pay for free, wide-reaching training, not paid courses. Plan B's free core and its enrollment numbers make CityUx a far stronger applicant, and the credential layer can later be expanded into a 150+ hour program for Workforce Pell.
Degrees
A designed path into CityU programs: the credential is assessed in the same format as STC courses, and CityU should look at granting it credit (for example toward the CS 11A/11B bridge courses). Completers get a named advisor and a tuition credit for a first course, which would need approval.

5 · Plan B phases and evidence gates

Each phase has to pass its gate before the next one gets money
  1. Phase 0
    Dec 2026 – Feb 2027
    Validation sprint, 10 weeks, about $8K. Build almost nothing. Instead:
    • 15 interviews with each of 2 audiences: K-12 teachers and principals (starting with School of Education alumni), and career changers (CityU alumni, office and admin staff, workers 50+, recent grads)
    • Meetings with 2–3 state agency training managers on how they meet WaTech's AI training requirement and how agencies buy training
    • 2 landing pages (educators, career changers) with waitlists and a $25 refundable seat reservation; for educators, test willingness to pay for clock hours
    • 3 free 90-minute live workshops: CityU faculty and staff (internal pilot), K-12 educators, and career changers
    • One interactive 3D teaching scene (for example, a classroom or office workflow) to test 3D as CityUx's teaching style across all X lenses
    • Talks with OSPI (clock-hour provider approval), with the School of Education (co-ownership of the educator lens), and with Career Bridge and the Workforce Board
    • A size estimate for each audience
    Gate 0: pick the 1–2 audiences that reach 150+ waitlist sign-ups, 20+ paid reservations, or a signed letter of intent from a partner (a school district, a state agency or an employer). Audiences that miss stop here, at the cost of a few weeks rather than a full course build.
  2. Phase 1
    Mar – Jun 2027
    Build and open. Release the free open core (4 modules) and the winning X lenses. Launch credential v1. Run 2 coached cohorts and roll out internal CityU staff training. Publish under CC BY, the same license as AI 410.
    Gate 1 (Jul 2027): 1,500+ free enrollments, 40%+ finishing module 1, 5%+ of core completers buying the credential or a cohort, 70%+ cohort completion.
  3. Phase 2
    Jul – Dec 2027
    Prove and sell. First private cohorts for employers and colleges, using CityU's own results as evidence. Add the next X lens based on traffic data. Run a first state agency cohort if a partner and a purchasing route are found. Offer OSPI clock hours on the educator lens once approved. Collect outcome data for the training list.
    Gate 2 (Jan 2028): 3+ paying organizations, measured enrollment into CityU programs, and a grant application submitted with the reach data.
  4. Phase 3
    2028
    Fund and scale. Microsoft Elevate or philanthropic partnership, a 150+ hour stackable program for Workforce Pell and the state training list, and more X lenses.

8 · Where Plan B could fail

Plan B gets the same critique as Plan A
WeaknessMitigation
Free learners rarely pay. Paid conversion could fall well under 5%.Private cohorts and grants carry the finances; the credential is a bonus. Test prices from $49 to $149 in Phase 1.
Slower revenue. Leadership may want income in 2027.Spending at risk is lower, and Gate 1 gives a clear revenue decision point in July 2027.
Free can look low-value.The accredited-university credential and live defense are the premium. Brand the core as "open," not "free."
Reach takes marketing. Open content nobody finds helps nobody.Start with CityU's own staff, students and alumni, then YouTube and WorkSource, which are already in the CityUx funnel.
Human scoring is hard to scale.Trained adjunct scorers, a rubric with examples, and a price that covers about $25 of scoring per attempt
The validation sprint delays the launch by about 3 months.That's intended. Three months of testing costs far less than a year spent on the wrong course.

10 · Recommendation

Take Plan B to stakeholders, testing two audiences from the target-market quadrant, K-12 educators and career changers, with state agencies explored through a partner. Plan A's higher-ed and Career Pivot designs become ready-made material for the X lenses. Plan B makes a smaller, verifiable promise ("in ten weeks we'll know which audience will pay, for about $8K"). That is easier to approve, fits the funnel the president already validated, and leaves CityUx with an evidence-based plan by March instead of a guess.

  • 1
    Approve the Phase 0 validation sprint (about $8K, December 2026 to February 2027) and its pass or fail criteria.
  • 2
    Approve an open license (CC BY) for the AI Fluency for X core, consistent with the CityUx open-source model.
  • 3
    Give permission to contact CityU alumni and staff for interviews and the pilot workshops, and ask the School of Education to co-own the educator lens and open its alumni network.
  • 4
    Ask the registrar and academic affairs to look into credit for the assessed credential and a completer tuition credit.
  • 5
    Start the OSPI clock-hour provider, Career Bridge and Workforce Pell conversations now, plus introductions to state agency training managers, alongside the sprint rather than blocking it.
