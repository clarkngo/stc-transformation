2 · If not the trades, then who?

Keep what made the trades idea work, and apply it where CityU is already trusted

The plumbing idea had the right business model in the wrong field. Its strength was required, recurring training: every plumber has to earn CE hours to renew a license. Its weakness was that CityU has no standing with plumbers. Two Washington markets have the same built-in demand and a reason to trust CityU:

1 · Teachers
K-12 educators renewing their certificates. Most Washington five-year teaching certificates require 100 clock hours to renew, and providers other than districts must be on OSPI's approved list. In a 2026 survey, 42% of U.S. teachers said they'd had no training on using AI in the classroom. CityU's School of Education already prepares Washington teachers, so this is CityU's home ground, much as the trades are a training center's.
2 · State staff
Washington state agency employees. WaTech's AI policy requires agencies to give every employee role-based AI awareness training, with new hires finishing it within 60 days of starting. There are about 76,500 executive-branch employees. Agencies choose their own training, so the payer is clear and the demand repeats. CityU would need a partner or a way into the state's procurement process, which makes this a partner play rather than a quick win.
3 · Career change
The other strong area is career change, which combines four groups from the quadrant that share a need and a course: career changers, office and administrative staff (the most AI-exposed jobs), workers over 50, and recent grads. About 176,000 Washingtonians were unemployed in September 2026 (not seasonally adjusted). The weak point is that these learners usually pay for themselves, and many can't. Plan B's free core plus paid credential fits them better than a paid course does.
Don't throw away the 3D work. Interactive 3D (pipes inside a wall, a hospital workflow, a classroom seating plan) can become CityUx's teaching approach across every X version, rather than a plumbing product.

4 · How the scores work

Market attractiveness (vertical)
The average of four 1–5 scores. Size: people in Washington. Urgency: how much AI or the job market is hurting them now. Clear payer: whether an employer, agency or fund will pay, rather than the learner. Repeat demand: required renewal training or a steady flow of new hires.
CityU's ability to win (horizontal)
The average of four 1–5 scores. Access: whether CityU already reaches these people. Credibility: whether they'd trust CityU on this. Competition gap: whether the free options leave room. Reuse: how much existing STC and CityU material we can use.
These are judgment scores, backed by the evidence in Section 5, not measurements. They exist to choose which audiences to test in Phase 0, not which ones to build for. Changing one score by one point moves a bubble by a quarter of a unit, so audiences near the dashed lines (state agency staff, small business, career changers) could fall in either quadrant.

6 · The three segments to test first

1 · AI Fluency for Educators (K-12), with clock hours

WhyRequired renewal hours, a big training gap, and CityU's School of Education is a trusted name with teachers
OfferFree open core plus an educator lens (lesson design, AI rules for each activity, academic integrity, student data privacy). Paid: OSPI clock hours and an assessed credential; district cohorts.
Watch outDistricts give clock hours for free, and Microsoft Elevate is training WA districts at no cost. Win on quality and on credential value, and work with the School of Education rather than around it.
Test in Phase 0Ask OSPI about provider approval; interview 15 teachers and principals (CityU School of Education alumni); test teachers' willingness to pay for clock hours

2 · AI Fluency for Your Next Job (career changers, admin staff, 50+, recent grads)

WhyThe most urgent need, CityU's core working-adult audience, an alumni base CityU can reach directly, and a path into STC programs
OfferFree open core plus starting-point lenses (office and admin, mid-career and 50+, early career). Paid: $99 assessed credential and $249 coached cohort, free for unemployed learners when a grant covers it.
Watch outLearners can't pay much. Grants and employers' outplacement budgets have to carry the cost.
Test in Phase 0Alumni survey and 15 interviews, a WorkSource info session, a landing page with refundable reservations

3 · Role-based AI training for Washington state agencies (partner play)

WhyA mandated, repeating, budgeted need: about 76,500 employees plus every new hire
OfferA role-based awareness course mapped to WaTech's policy and data categories, delivered as agency cohorts
Watch outProcurement is slow, and CityU has few ties to state agencies yet. Explore the DES training catalog or a partner that already holds a state contract.
Test in Phase 0Meet 2–3 agency training managers; check how agencies currently meet the requirement; check the procurement route

Higher-ed staff stay as the internal proving ground: CityU's own faculty and staff pilot the core first. The trades, veterans and nonprofit staff are parked. Health care and small business are worth revisiting if a partner appears.
