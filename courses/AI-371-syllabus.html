Course Description

Moves beyond traditional Cyber Ethics to address rapidly evolving AI challenges: algorithmic bias, automated decision-making, generative AI ethics, data equity, digital surveillance, the “alignment problem,” and AI governance.

Course Outcomes

As a result of this course students will acquire course outcomes and demonstrate them through their assignments:

  • CO1: Explain fundamental ethical frameworks (Deontology, Utilitarianism, Virtue Ethics) and how they apply to both human cyber-behavior and AI logic.

  • CO2: Analyze sources of algorithmic bias, data discrimination in ML pipelines, and AI-driven misinformation impacting civil liberties.

  • CO3: Apply professional ethical codes to resolve conflicts related to privacy, security, and automated decision-making.

  • CO4: Evaluate ethical implications of autonomous systems regarding accountability when an AI agent causes harm.

  • CO5: Develop a comprehensive Ethical AI Framework incorporating data transparency, model explainability, and human-in-the-loop oversight.

The Muddiest Point (MP) (10% of Final Grade)

Before class, students are required to submit the Muddiest Point (MP) activity. The MP consists of writing a brief reflective essay identifying the most confusing part (i.e., the MP) of the content covered in the upcoming module. If a student understands all concepts, the student needs to explain the most exciting aspect. The purpose of this activity is to stimulate student engagement. The instructor uses the MP to assess how students understood the required readings. The instructor also uses the MP to customize the lecture scope to implement Just-in-Time Teaching (JiTT).

Team Project (TP) – Cyber and AI Ethics – Ethical AI Framework Development (20% of Final Grade)

Each student team will conduct an in-depth case analysis for a selected case which describes unethical behaviors related to Information Security or an AI system (for example, a biased automated decision, an AI-driven misinformation campaign, unchecked surveillance, or an AI agent acting without adequate oversight). Each case will need to be approved by the instructor before being accepted. For each case, the team will provide an analysis which describes the breakdown of ethical behaviors.

The team should differentiate between legal unethical behaviors and illegal behaviors and explain how the initial lapses led to more serious behavior. The student will describe how Information and Communications Technology (ICT) and AI impacted ethical behaviors and situations. The student will outline controls, education, and environmental factors which may have prevented ethical breaches or minimized consequences, or both.

Building on that analysis, the team will develop an Ethical AI Framework for the organization in the case. The framework must address, at minimum: data transparency (what data is used and where it comes from), model explainability (how decisions can be explained to the people they affect), human-in-the-loop oversight (where a person reviews, overrides, or stops the system), accountability (who answers when the system causes harm), and alignment with a professional code and at least one governance standard (e.g., the NIST AI RMF or the EU AI Act).

The analysis of the case should include proper documentation of the case, including news articles or articles providing supporting information. All documentation should use APA standards.

Design notes — what we took from IS 471 (planning only)

This section is for course designers and is not part of the student syllabus. Remove it before the Brightspace copy, along with the yellow highlighting.

Verdict: copy IS 471, change only what the AAC outcomes require

The IS 471 syllabus (effective 4/1/2025) and schedule (8/28/2026) were reviewed. Their structure maps well onto AI 371: same credits, no prerequisite, the same five-outcome shape (CO5 “create an ethics program” becomes “develop an Ethical AI Framework”), and Module 10 of the schedule already ends on Responsible AI Governance. So the assignment types, weights, points, rubrics, TP phases, textbook, and module sequence are all kept.
