Course description

IS 471AI 371 (AAC-approved, quoted word for word)
Cyber Ethics introduces students to the social, ethical, and policy dimensions of technology in organizations, schools, and society. This course examines access and equity issues, censorship, privacy, piracy, hacking, commercialization, literacy, online communication, intellectual property, crime, civil liberties, social media, and developing a “global community” through the Internet. It covers the students' understanding of professional and ethical responsibilities, and awareness of ethical issues facing IT professionals is emphasized. It explores the analysis of situations involving ethical conflicts and the ability to make decisions based on professional codes of ethics and conduct is explored.Moves beyond traditional Cyber Ethics to address rapidly evolving AI challenges: algorithmic bias, automated decision-making, generative AI ethics, data equity, digital surveillance, the “alignment problem,” and AI governance.

Team project

IS 471AI 371
In-depth analysis of a case of unethical behavior related to information security: legal-but-unethical vs. illegal behavior, how lapses escalated, how ICT shaped the situation, and the controls, education, and environmental factors that could have prevented it.Same analysis, widened to cases involving an AI system. Added: the team develops an Ethical AI Framework for the organization covering data transparency, model explainability, human-in-the-loop oversight, accountability, and alignment with a professional code and a governance standard (O5).

Fixes to the source

  • IS 471's assignment headings said MP 5% and RP 40%, but its grade table said 10% and 30% (which sum to 100%); the table's numbers are used.

  • IS 471 called the labs both “Labs” and “Virtual Lab (VL)”; Virtual Lab (VL) is used throughout.
