AI 410 — Delivery Walkthrough

AI 410 was redesigned from 8 individually-graded weekly skills into 4 merged Hands-On Skill (HOS) units, graded on a new CEAU cycle (Create/Scaffold → Evaluate → Analyze → Understand & Refine) instead of code correctness alone — reflecting a course where students use an AI assistant to scaffold code, then have to prove they understand what it built. Improved Muddiest Point & Concept Debate

The two problems flagged in practice: MP submissions were low-effort ("people just post whatever"), and Concept Debate responses were collapsing into "I agree." Both are now research-backed, structured formats, added directly to the syllabus.

New

Muddiest Point — Structured, Standing Submission

Based on ACQUIRED (2025): a standing, always-open submission (not one Friday form) with 4 required fields — what you were doing, where you got stuck, what you tried, and an optional guess. A low-effort post is structurally harder to write than a real one.

See it in the syllabus →
New

Concept Debate — Argue Both Sides, Then Synthesize

Based on Mitchell (2019) and Structured Academic Controversy: an assigned (not chosen) position, followed by arguing the opposite side before any response is allowed — breaking "I agree" as a viable reply since nobody's own position is left to agree with.

See it in the syllabus →
New

Debate Sides via Brightspace Teams

Sides are assigned without faculty effort: Brightspace randomly and evenly splits the class into two debate teams in Week 1, and the sides rotate each HOS. A fallback rule covers late adds.

See the Week 1 setup →

Team/Term Project Support

Per the Dean's requirement, each HOS now runs two weeks (Weeks 1–8), which leaves only Weeks 9–10 for the project itself. Its checkpoint and architecture/integration review are folded into the ends of Weeks 6 and 8 instead of claiming weeks of their own, and it's introduced back in Week 1 so students carry an idea the whole term — plus an example project, a Scrum-lite framework, and PM tool guidance so "plan your project" isn't left undefined even with just two dedicated weeks.

New

Timeline & Weekly Milestones

Week 1: introduced. Week 6 (end of HOS 3): team + one-paragraph idea. Week 8 (end of HOS 4): architecture/integration review. Week 9: scope locked, then build begins. Week 10: feature-complete, rehearsed, and demoed.

See it in the syllabus →
New

Example Project & Scrum-lite Framework

A worked example ("Study Buddy," an AI study assistant touching all 4 HOS) plus a 4-piece Scrum-lite framework (backlog, sprint planning, standups, sprint review) and a free PM-tool comparison (GitHub Projects, Trello, Notion), compressed into the first days of Week 9.

See It From a Student's Seat

All 10 weeks, simulated as "Module" pages — reassembling the real course content in the order and format a student would actually encounter it. Each HOS now spans two modules (build/evaluate, then analyze/master), with the project checkpoint folded into Module 6 and the architecture/integration review folded into Module 8 — plus illustrative Langfuse trace/score mockups in Module 7, where the Evaluate stage actually uses that dashboard. Built to review pacing and tone from the student's side, not ours.
