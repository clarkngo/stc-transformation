Lecture / Pre-work
Embeddings, Chunking & Retrieval-Augmented Generation
~20 min

Covers what an embedding actually is (a list of numbers representing meaning), why documents get chunked before embedding, and how RAG grounds an answer in retrieved text instead of the model's own memory. Watch before starting your Muddiest Point below.

Discussion — Concept Debate
Should a RAG assistant always say "I don't know" when its retrieved context doesn't cover a question — or is it fine to fill the gap with the model's own general knowledge?
5% of grade

You've been assigned a side below (not your choice) — small groups, staged across the week:

Stage 1 — Initial position (Mon–Tue): Argue your assigned side with a specific claim and evidence.
Stage 2 — Argue the opposite (Wed–Thu): Post the strongest version of the other side's argument before responding to a classmate.
Stage 3 — Synthesis (Fri): Drop advocacy. State a genuine synthesis, citing a specific point from a classmate's post.

Your assigned side this week: Group A — Strict grounding ("I don't know" is always correct when context is missing).

Hands-On Skill (HOS) Build
HOS 2 — Embeddings, Chunking & RAG Pipelines
12.5% of grade~2–2.5 hrs

Full guide: HOS 2 Hands-On Guide → — starter code, exact prompts, and submission details.

1. Create/Scaffold — prompt an AI assistant to chunk/embed sample documents into a vector database and wire retrieval into your Module 1 agent.
2. Evaluate — test a document-grounded question, a question needing your Module 1 tool (does RAG silently break tool-calling?), and a question the docs don't cover.
3. Analyze — explain why ingest.py and retrieval.py use different task_type values for the same embedding API.
4. Understand & Refine — by hand: write a sixth sample document and confirm a question about it returns the right answer.

Application Challenge
Evaluate an unfamiliar RAG pipeline
5% of grade

Given a different, unfamiliar RAG pipeline's code, evaluate it: identify one thing it gets right, one thing that's likely to quietly underperform (not crash — just answer worse), and explain how you'd confirm your suspicion without running it.
