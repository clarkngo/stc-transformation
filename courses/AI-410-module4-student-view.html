Mobile Clients, Push Notifications & Observability
~20 min

Covers how a mobile client (Expo/React Native) calls the same backend your other clients do, how a push notification reaches a specific device via a push token, and why tracing a request as nested spans (retrieval, generation) lets you answer "which part was actually slow?" Discussion — Concept Debate
Should an AI product proactively alert users the moment observability data suggests something might be wrong — or only surface issues when a user explicitly asks?
5% of grade

You've been assigned a side below (not your choice) — small groups, staged across the week:

Stage 1 — Initial position (Mon–Tue): Argue your assigned side with a specific claim and evidence.
Stage 2 — Argue the opposite (Wed–Thu): Post the strongest version of the other side's argument before responding to a classmate.
Stage 3 — Synthesis (Fri): Drop advocacy. State a genuine synthesis, citing a specific point from a classmate's post.

Your assigned side this week: Group A — Proactive alerts, even at the risk of alert fatigue.

Hands-On Skill (HOS) Build
HOS 4 — Mobile Client, Push & Observability
12.5% of grade~2.5–3 hrs

Full guide: HOS 4 Hands-On Guide → — starter code, exact prompts, and submission details.

1. Create/Scaffold — prompt an AI assistant to add an Expo mobile client, device push registration, and Langfuse tracing.
2. Evaluate — confirm a push notification actually arrives on your phone, open a real trace and check for two separate spans, and check a faithfulness score against a question the docs don't cover.
3. Analyze — explain why push notifications are addressed to a device token, or why retrieval and generation are two nested spans instead of one flat one.
4. Understand & Refine — by hand: write your own score_faithfulness() function and log it against a real trace.
