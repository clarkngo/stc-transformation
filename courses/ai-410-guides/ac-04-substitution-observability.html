Application Challenge 4 — Puget Pantry Substitution Observability

Est. 60–90 minOutcomes 4 & 5After HOS 4 · end of Week 8IndividualAI assistant allowed — disclose it5% of final grade
This challenge follows the BSAI course design constraints: it adapts to AI 410's official Outcomes 4 and 5, runs entirely in GitHub Codespaces, and uses only free tiers. Everything runs offline: no API key, Langfuse project, or phone needed.

Goal

Take the tracing and scoring skills from HOS 4 to someone else's AI feature: instrument it so every order shows what happened at each step, score each AI decision, and decide what's worth waking the on-call engineer for.

The scenario

Puget Pantry (fictional) delivers groceries. When an item in an order is out of stock, an AI feature picks a replacement from the same category and pushes the change to the customer's phone. It's been in production for a month. It works, and nobody can see inside it.

You get the feature (four steps: look up stock, find candidates, ask the model, notify), the catalog, the customers and their allergies, and one day of orders.

  • The model is recorded. fake_model.py replays what the model chose in a test run, so everyone's traces match. Like the real model, it isn't told about allergies or price.
  • The tracer is shaped like Langfuse (HOS 4): traces, spans inside them, and scores attached. It records in memory instead of sending anywhere.

The spec

Every requirement below is checked by a provided test in tests/test_instrumented.py. Your code goes in instrumented.py.

FunctionMust do
score_substitution(original, substitute, customer)0.0 for no substitute, or one with an allergen the customer listed; 0.5 if it costs more than 20% above the original; otherwise 1.0.
run_order(order)One trace per order. A lookup_stock span, one choose_substitute span per out-of-stock item (the item, how many candidates, what was chosen, how long the model took), and a notify span with the message, when anything was swapped. One quality score per swap. The customer gets exactly what the untraced feature would send.
should_page(trace)True or False for every order. An order with nothing out of stock never pages.

The open decision: what pages the on-call engineer

The operations lead: "Page us if substitutions go wrong. But if the pager goes off for nothing, people start ignoring it, and then it's useless." Nobody said what "go wrong" means.

Write should_page. Everything that doesn't page belongs on a dashboard. Prove your rule with your own tests and defend it in DECISIONS.md.

What the trace lets you see

In your memo, break the feature into its four steps. For each problem in the day's report (a quality 0.0, a quality 0.5, the slow model call), say which span shows it, and what you'd have known from one "order processed" log line instead. Then name one thing the feature gets wrong that your trace still can't show.

Steps

1Open it and run the tests

Open the AC 4 starter-code folder in GitHub Codespaces. It installs everything for you. Then run:

pytest

Almost everything fails. That's your to-do list.

2Score a substitution

Write score_substitution from the spec. You may use an AI assistant; read what it writes, because you'll defend it.

3Trace an order

Write run_order: call the feature's four steps yourself, each inside its span, and attach the scores. Don't change what the customer gets: test_tracing_does_not_change_what_customers_get checks.

4Make the paging call

Write should_page and at least two tests in tests/test_my_rules.py: an order from the day that must page and one that must not, plus the case closest to your rule. You can build a trace by hand for a case the day doesn't contain.

5Run the day

Run every order through your instrumented feature:

python run_day.py

Paste the output into REPORT.md and fill in DECISIONS.md.

Submit (via Brightspace)
  • instrumented.py and tests/test_my_rules.py
  • The output of pytest, pasted as text (all tests passing, including yours)
  • REPORT.md
  • DECISIONS.md

How this differs from HOS 4's Understand & Refine: there you wrote a faithfulness score for your own app, by hand. Here the feature, its failures, and the paging call are someone else's, AI help is allowed, and half the grade (Analysis and Evaluation) is for the calls you make.

Common snags

  • The span order is wrong. Open each span with with trace.span(...) right before the step it measures. Don't create them all at the end.
  • test_tracing_does_not_change_what_customers_get fails. You're calling notify once per item, or calling run_order_plain as well. Collect the swaps, then notify once.
  • Oat milk scores 1.0. 4.29 against 3.49 is 23% more, so it's a 0.5. Compare against the original price times 1.2.
  • Everything pages. That's the failure the operations lead warned about. Re-read the open decision.
Faculty notes: what strong answers look like, and grading

The open decision

Several rules are defensible. Grade the reasoning, not the choice.

  • Page only when an allergen was pushed to the customer (the answer key): O-502, O-505, O-506. Rare, can hurt someone, and someone can act now (call the customer, stop the delivery).
  • Page on any quality 0.0: the same orders today, but it would also page for "no substitute found", which isn't urgent. Strong answers notice the score lumps the two together.
  • Page on a slow model call (O-505, O-507 at 3,900 ms): usually weak. Slowness is a dashboard trend unless orders are failing.

Typical answers page on everything that looks bad (price jumps, slow calls, low averages), which is exactly what the lead said makes people ignore the pager.

The analysis

  • Which span shows what: allergen problems show in choose_substitute (what was chosen) plus the customer's allergies; the price jump shows in the score; the slow call shows in model_ms. One log line would show only "order processed" for all ten.
  • Strong answers point out that the push is sent before anyone could check: by the time the allergen page fires, the customer already has the message. That's a design flaw the trace reveals but can't fix.
  • What the trace can't show: whether the customer accepted or returned the substitute, or whether a cheaper, safe candidate existed (the span records the count, not the options).

The recorded day: 10 orders, 13 substitutions, 8 pushes, average quality 0.58 (three 0.0s, five 0.5s, five 1.0s). A reference submission is in the answer key, with steps to check it.

Grading quickly (about 8–10 minutes per student)

Each criterion maps to one part of the submission, so read each part once, in this order, and pick a level:

  1. Design (40): the pasted pytest output, then test_my_rules.py (is there a hand-built edge case?).
  2. Application (10): REPORT.md. Do the pages match the rule the student describes?
  3. Evaluation (30): memo section 1.
  4. Analysis (20): memo section 2.

You don't need to run the code. In Brightspace, use the rubric above with levels (Full = 100%, Partial = 50%, Missing = 0% of the criterion).
