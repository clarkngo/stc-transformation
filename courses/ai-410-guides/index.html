AI 410: Hands-On Guides — Week by Week

Ten step-by-step guides, one per week of the AI 410 schedule. Each guide expands that week's "App Milestone" into concrete steps: what you're starting from, what to build, and how to know it worked. They build on each other — the app you extend in Week 8 is the same one you started in Week 1.

Each week also has real starter code on GitHub — a self-contained folder you can copy straight into your own project. See the "Code →" link on each row below, or browse starter-code/ for all ten at once.

Why This Matters

This isn't a tutorial project — it's the job

"AI Engineer" is one of the fastest-growing job titles in software right now, and it means something specific: not prompting ChatGPT well, but building the systems around a model that make it reliable enough to put in front of real users — retrieval so it answers from real data instead of guessing, guardrails so bad output doesn't crash the app, background processing so it doesn't freeze under load, and monitoring so someone finds out about a problem before a customer does. Every one of those is a separate week in this schedule, because every one of them is a separate skill companies are hiring for.

By Week 10, you have something to show, not just something to submit

Most course projects live and die on a grader's screen. This one doesn't: it's a live, deployed, agent-driven mobile app you can hand someone a link to, and a GitHub repo with real commit history showing how it was built, one working piece at a time. In an interview, "I followed a tutorial" and "I built this, here's why I made this specific tradeoff in Week 6" are very different answers — this schedule is built to get you to the second one.

6Guardrails for nondeterministic outputThe exact gap between a demo that works when you type the happy-path question, and a product that survives real users typing whatever they want.
7Asynchronous processingA core backend skill that has nothing to do with AI specifically — any app that uploads files, sends emails, or does anything slow uses this same queue/worker pattern.
8Mobile client & push notificationsProof you can work across the whole stack, not just one layer of it — most teams want engineers who aren't siloed to "just backend" or "just frontend."
9Observability & evaluationWhat companies mean by "AI evals" — a newly emerging, actively-hiring specialization built on one simple idea: you can't improve a system you can't measure.
10DeploymentThe single biggest gap between "I can code" and "I can ship" — code that only runs on your own laptop isn't a product yet, and most student projects never cross that line.
