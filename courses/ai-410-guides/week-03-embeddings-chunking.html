Week 3 — Embeddings & Chunking

Est. 3–4 hrs Outcome 1 Sandbox — not wired to the live app yet

Goal

Build intuition for embeddings and chunking in isolation, in a sandbox script, before connecting anything to your live app next week. Confirm — with a real similarity score, not just a hunch — that your chunking choices actually group related content together.

What you'll need

  • The sandbox notebook/script from your instructor
  • A folder of 5–10 short sample documents (text or markdown)
  • A Voyage AI API key for embeddings (Anthropic's recommended embeddings partner — Claude itself doesn't serve embeddings)

Steps

  1. Skim the sample docs first

    Open 2–3 of them so you know what content you're about to chunk — you'll need this context to judge whether your results look right later.

  2. Write a chunking function

    Split each document into roughly 300–500 token pieces, with a little overlap between consecutive chunks so you don't cut a sentence's meaning in half at a boundary. Start by splitting on paragraphs, then fall back to a character-count split for any paragraph that's too long on its own.

  3. Embed each chunk

    import voyageai
vo = voyageai.Client()

result = vo.embed(chunks, model="voyage-3", input_type="document")
vectors = result.embeddings   # one vector per chunk, same order
    Keep the vectors and their original chunk text together in a plain Python list — no database yet, that's Week 4.

  4. Sanity-check with cosine similarity

    Pick two chunks you'd expect to be related and two you'd expect to be unrelated. Compute cosine similarity between their vectors and confirm the related pair scores meaningfully higher. 
    import numpy as np

def cosine_sim(a, b):
    a, b = np.array(a), np.array(b)
    return a.dot(b) / (np.linalg.norm(a) * np.linalg.norm(b))

  5. Run a mini retrieval test

    Embed a short query the same way, compute its similarity against every chunk, and print the top 3 by score. By eye, is the top result genuinely the most relevant chunk to that query?

  6. Record your settings

    Note your chunk size, overlap amount, and total chunk count somewhere — you'll load these same chunks into a real vector database next week.

You'll know it worked when…

  • Your similarity scores clearly separate related from unrelated chunks — not a coin flip.
  • A ranked-by-similarity list for a sample query puts the genuinely relevant chunk at or near the top.

Common snags

  • Chunks too large — everything starts looking similarly related because each chunk covers too many topics at once.
  • Chunks too small — individual chunks lose the surrounding context needed to make sense on their own.
  • Hitting the embeddings API's per-request token limit — batch your chunks into smaller groups per call if you have a large sample set.
