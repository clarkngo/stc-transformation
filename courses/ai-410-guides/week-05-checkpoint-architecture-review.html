Week 5 — Checkpoint: Architecture & Bottleneck Review

No new build this week Est. 2–3 hrs Outcome 3

Goal

Step back before the two hardest weeks of the course. Diagram the app exactly as it stands after Weeks 1–4, and name — in writing, with specifics — where it can actually break.

What you'll need

  • Starter code: the review template — copy it and fill it in
  • Your working app from Weeks 1–4 (running locally is enough — you're observing it, not changing it)
Example only — the point of Step 1 is drawing your own version of this.

Steps

  1. Draw the request path end to end

    The diagram above is a generic example of the shape — your job is to draw the same kind of thing for your own code, as it actually exists today (not how you wish it worked). You don't need any special software: a piece of paper and a pencil is completely fine, or a free tool like excalidraw.com if you'd rather do it digitally. Boxes for each step, arrows for what calls what.
    How to figure out your actual path: open backend/agent.py and read it top to bottom, in the order the code actually executes. Every function call you see (retrieve(), client.interactions.create(), your tool functions) is a box; the order they happen in is your arrows.

  2. Interrogate every arrow

    For each arrow in your diagram, ask two questions and write one sentence each: what happens if this step is slow? and what happens if this step returns something malformed or unexpected? Don't skip steps that feel "boring" (like the frontend sending the message) — bugs hide in the steps nobody thinks to question.

  3. Name at least 3 concrete failure points

    Not generic risks like "the API could be down" — specific ones tied to an exact place in your code. See the worked examples in the table below for what "specific" looks like.

  4. Propose a mitigation for each

    One sentence per failure point. You're not building the fix yet (Week 6 is where the guardrails one gets built) — just committing to a direction, so you have something concrete to act on later.

  5. Present it live

    Walk the instructor through your diagram and findings out loud. Saying it aloud surfaces gaps that re-reading your own notes doesn't — you'll often catch yourself mid-sentence realizing a "mitigation" doesn't actually address the failure you named.

You'll know it worked when…

  • You have a written Architecture & Bottleneck Review with a diagram and at least 3 named failure points, each tied to a specific place in your own app and a specific proposed mitigation.
  • You've reviewed it live with the instructor, not just submitted it.

Tips

  • "The API could go down" is not specific enough. "If the embeddings API is down, retrieve() throws and the whole /chat request 500s instead of just skipping context" is.
  • You'll use this list directly in Week 6 (guardrails) and Week 7 (async processing) — don't treat it as a one-off assignment.
