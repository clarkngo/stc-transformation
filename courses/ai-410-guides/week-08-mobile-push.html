Week 8 — Mobile Client & Real-Time Push Notifications

Est. 3–4 hrs Outcome 5 Runs on your own phone

Goal

Get your app running on your own physical phone, and receive a real push notification the moment a Week 7 background job finishes — no Xcode, no Android Studio, no emulator.

What you'll need

  • Starter code: this week's starter-code folder — includes the Expo mobile shell and the backend's device-registration endpoint
  • The Expo Go app installed on your personal phone
  • Node.js on your laptop

Steps

  1. Start the Expo dev server

    cd mobile
npm install
npx expo start
    A QR code appears in your terminal.

  2. Open the app on your phone

    Scan the QR code — with your Camera app on iOS, or directly in Expo Go on Android. The app opens live on your phone, running your actual code, not a build.

  3. Confirm the basics work first

    The starter's mobile chat screen is already wired to your backend. Send a message and confirm you get a reply — the same backend you've been building since Week 1, just a different client.

  4. Request notification permission

    The starter includes the expo-notifications setup already; run the app and accept the permission prompt when it appears.

  5. Register your device's push token

    The starter's POST /register-device endpoint stores the token your phone generates — confirm it's actually landing in your backend (check your logs or database).

  6. Trigger a push from a backend event

    In your Week 7 job function, after ingestion finishes, add one call to Expo's push API using the stored token: 
    import requests

requests.post("https://exp.host/--/api/v2/push/send", json={
    "to": push_token,
    "title": "Document ready",
    "body": "Your document has finished processing.",
})

  7. Test it for real

    Upload a document from the mobile app, then lock your phone or switch to another app. When the Week 7 background job finishes, a real push notification should appear on your lock screen.

You'll know it worked when…

  • You get an actual push notification on your own physical phone.
  • You can trace it back to the exact backend event (a finished job) that triggered it.

Common snags

  • Push notifications don't fire in a simulator/emulator — this has to be a real physical device for the free Expo Go flow.
  • Phone can't reach your laptop's backend — make sure both are on the same Wi-Fi network, or use Expo's tunnel mode if they're not.
  • Permission denied — if you accidentally declined the notification prompt, you'll need to re-enable it in your phone's system settings for Expo Go.
