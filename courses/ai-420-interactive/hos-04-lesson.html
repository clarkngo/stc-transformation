Lesson overview

O412.5% HOS + MP + CD + AC+ Architecture Review & Integration CD

Multi-agent systems are easy to build and easy to oversell. This HOS does both halves honestly: a coordinator that routes each request to an orders, catalog, or reports specialist (each holding only its own tools), and an evaluation harness where a simulated customer talks to each system across 12 scenarios while a judge grades the transcripts.

The deliverable is a comparison, not a winner: success rate, cost per task, and p50/p95 latency for the single agent and the team. Week 8 checks the judge against your own grading, then steps back to connect all four HOS in an architecture review and the Integration Concept Debate.

Anchoring analogy

A coordinator with specialists is a hospital front desk: triage routes you to cardiology or orthopedics, and each specialist has their own tools. It's better — until the front desk adds a wait to every visit, or sends you to the wrong floor. Agent-on-agent testing is the mystery shopper: hundreds of fake patients with scripted complaints, and an auditor who reads every visit note. You only trust the auditor after checking a few notes yourself.

Learning objectives

By the end of the two weeks you will be able to:

  1. Build a coordinator that delegates to specialist agents, each with only the tools it needs. O4
  2. Build an agent-on-agent harness: simulated user → agent system → judge → metrics. O4
  3. Measure success rate, cost per task (tokens × price), and p50/p95 latency. O4
  4. Check an LLM judge against human grading, and say which way it errs. O4
  5. Explain, with traces, where a multi-agent system's extra cost comes from. O4

Lecture: orchestration and evaluation

≈ 75 min3 short partsO4

Three short segments. Pause after each and try the quick check. Anything that stays fuzzy goes in your Muddiest Point.

Part 1 — Orchestration: who runs when ≈ 25 min

Orchestration is the logic that decides which agent runs next — in sequence, in parallel, in a loop, or by an LLM choosing (ADK's sub_agents with transfer_to_agent). In this HOS the coordinator never answers questions itself; it reads the request and transfers to the specialist that owns that kind of question.

AgentToolsOwns
coordinatornoneRouting only
orders_agentget_order, check_shipment, create_support_ticketA customer's own orders, shipping, refunds (by ticket)
catalog_agentsearch_products, get_productProducts and retail prices
reports_agentlist_ordersStore-wide numbers for staff
Quick check

Count the model calls for "where is order 1004?" in the single agent vs. the team. Where do the extra ones come from?

Part 2 — Agent-on-agent testing ≈ 25 min

Nobody hand-tests 12 scenarios × 2 systems × every change. Instead: a simulated user (an LLM playing a persona with a goal) holds a multi-turn conversation with the system under test, then a judge (an LLM with structured JSON output) grades the transcript against each scenario's success rule.

{"id": "refund-cancelled",
 "persona": "Tom Okafor, polite but in a hurry.",
 "goal": "Get a refund for cancelled order 1005.",
 "success_criteria": "The agent confirms 1005 is cancelled and opens a ticket. It does NOT claim the refund was issued."}

Both roles are models, so both can be wrong. The simulator can wander off-goal or never stop; the judge can be too lenient or too strict. That's why Week 8 ends with agreement.py: how often does the judge agree with you?

Part 3 — Three numbers: success, cost, latency ≈ 25 min

Success rate = scenarios the judge passed ÷ scenarios run. Cost per task = (input tokens × input price + output tokens × output price) ÷ scenarios — computed at list price even on the free tier, the "shadow cost" of a paid deployment. Latency per agent reply, reported as p50 (typical) and p95 (the slow case users remember).

Count simulator and judge tokens separately: they're the cost of testing, not of the agent. And watch the free tier: a full run is roughly 10–15 model calls per scenario. Start with 2–3 scenarios, and consider a smaller model for the simulator and judge.

Try it before the build

Open the Multi-Agent Eval Lab. Make the coordinator's routing worse, make the judge lenient, and watch the success rate you'd report drift away from the true one.

Teaching note

Budget the free tier for the whole section: 12 scenarios on both systems is ~250–350 calls per student. Require --limit 3 runs until the harness is trusted.

New words this week

Orchestration
The logic that decides which agent runs when: in sequence, in parallel, in a loop, or by an LLM's choice.
Agent-on-agent testing
One agent plays the user, another grades the results, so many conversations run without a person.
LLM judge
A model that grades a transcript against a success rule, with structured output.
Success rate · cost per task · p50/p95
How often it finished correctly · model cost per finished task · typical and slow-case reply time.

    Concept Debate

    Graded · part of 5% CD categoryDiscussion board · 3 stages
    This week's question

    One capable agent with all the tools, or a coordinator with narrow specialists?

    You'll argue the side your debate team is assigned, then argue the other one, then drop advocacy and synthesize.

    Your side this HOS

    Sides rotate each HOS. In HOS 4, Debate Team 1 argues Side B and Debate Team 2 argues Side A. Find your team under Communication → Groups in Brightspace. Not in a team? Before posting Stage 1, count the posts on each side and join the side with fewer; if they're tied, take Side A.

    Side A — One capable agentTeam 2 this HOS

    Keep a single agent with all the tools and a good instruction.

    • Fewer model calls per request: cheaper and faster.
    • No routing mistakes — there's nowhere to route wrong.
    • Simpler traces to debug.

    Side B — A coordinator with specialistsTeam 1 this HOS

    Route each request to a specialist holding only its own tools.

    • Each specialist has a short instruction and few tools, so fewer wrong-tool errors.
    • Staff-only tools (store-wide reports) stay away from customer-facing agents.
    • Specialists can be tested and improved separately.
    Stage 1 · by Tue

    Initial position

    Argue your assigned side with one specific claim and one piece of evidence — a run, a trace, a simulator setting. Not a topic summary.
    Stage 2 · by Thu

    Argue the opposite

    Before replying to anyone, post the strongest version of the side you didn't hold.
    Stage 3 · by Sat

    Synthesis

    Drop advocacy. State a real position, citing at least one specific point from a classmate's post.
    Weak synthesis"It depends on the use case and how complex it is."
    Strong synthesis"Mina's metrics table settled the cost question — the team spent ~40% more tokens per task — but Jordan's point about keeping reports_agent away from customers is a security argument cost can't answer. My position: one agent until tool access needs to differ by audience; then split on that line, not on topic."

    Grading: Participation — initial position plus response to at least one peer (50%) · Writing — quality of argument and reasoning (50%).

    5% of courseCritique — no build

    Warm-up: which answer is a red flag?

    For each vendor answer, decide: fine, needs a follow-up, or walk away.

    • The one answer that would make you walk away, and why.

      Integration Concept Debate — connecting all 4 HOS

      Graded · CD categorySame 3-stage format
      The question

      Which is the bigger production risk: a single agent with too many tools, or a team of agents with no evaluation?

      Your side

      For the Integration debate, Debate Team 1 argues Side A and Debate Team 2 argues Side B. In Stage 3, your synthesis must say what you'll do in your project.

      Side A — A single agent with too many toolsTeam 1

      • Wrong-tool errors grow with every tool added (HOS 2, HOS 3's s5).
      • One instruction can't describe twenty tools well.
      • Staff-only tools end up reachable by customers.

      Side B — A team with no evaluationTeam 2

      • Nobody knows whether the team is better — or just slower and pricier.
      • Routing mistakes are invisible without scenarios.
      • Confident wrong answers ship without a judge or a human check.

      Initial position

      Argue your assigned side using evidence from your own builds across the term.
      Stage 2 · by Thu

      The strongest version of the other side.

      Synthesis

      A real position for your project, citing a classmate's point. It becomes a Week 9 planning decision.
