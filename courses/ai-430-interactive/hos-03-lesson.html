Lesson overview

O3 · O412.5% HOS + MP + CD + AC+ Team/Term Project checkpoint

A deployed model degrades quietly: the world changes and its inputs drift away from what it trained on. A deployed agent degrades differently: a model version changes, a document set grows, and its reasoning gets worse without an error anywhere. This HOS instruments both — drift reports for the model, traces with LLM-judge scores and latency for the agent — and benchmarks the RAG pipeline inside the agent for both speed and relevance.

Week 5 builds the monitoring and the benchmark. Week 6 explains what each signal can and can't tell you, adds a check by hand, and ends with the Team/Term Project checkpoint.

Anchoring analogy

A car's dashboard. The speedometer (latency) and the fuel gauge (cost) are easy. The hard lights are the ones that warn about slow problems before they're breakdowns — tire pressure (drift) and the engine light (reasoning accuracy). A dashboard with only a speedometer tells you you're going fast right up until the wheel comes off.

Learning objectives

By the end of the two weeks you will be able to:

  1. Implement monitoring that tracks model drift in near real time. O3
  2. Measure an agent's reasoning accuracy with LLM-based evaluation, and check the judge. O3
  3. Track system latency at p50 and p95 across the pipeline. O3
  4. Analyze a RAG pipeline's retrieval speed and context relevance within an agentic system. O4
  5. Choose index settings that trade speed against relevance, with evidence. O4

Lecture: signals that warn before things break

≈ 75 min3 short partsO3 · O4

Three short segments. Pause after each and try the quick check. Anything that stays fuzzy goes in your Muddiest Point.

Part 1 — Drift: when the world moves ≈ 25 min

Data drift means today's inputs don't look like the training data (a new customer segment, a sensor recalibrated). Concept drift means the right answer for the same input changed. Drift reports compare distributions — this week vs. the training set — and flag the features that moved. Proposed tool: Evidently.

Quick check

Accuracy looks fine, but one feature has drifted sharply. Should you retrain? What would you check first?

Part 2 — Reasoning accuracy, judges, and latency ≈ 25 min

For an agent, each run becomes a trace (AI 420 HOS 3) with an LLM-judge score attached: did the reasoning reach the right answer for the right reasons? Proposed tool: Langfuse (free), with the judge score logged on every trace and latency per span.

A judge is a model, so it needs checking: grade a sample yourself and measure agreement, exactly as AI 420 HOS 4 did. Report latency as p50 and p95 — and per span, so a slow retriever doesn't hide inside a slow total.

Part 3 — RAG performance: speed and relevance ≈ 25 min

Inside the agent, retrieval has two numbers that trade against each other: latency and context relevance (did the retrieved chunks contain what the answer needs?). Proposed tools: Chroma vs. Qdrant in Docker, measured with Ragas-style retrieval metrics.

Worked example

Raising top-k from 3 to 10 lifts relevance from 0.71 to 0.83 and adds 40 ms of retrieval — and 2,000 prompt tokens per request, which costs more than the retrieval itself. The benchmark has to report all three.

New words this week

Data drift vs. concept drift
Inputs changing vs. the right answer changing.
Reasoning accuracy
How often an agent's reasoning reaches the right answer for the right reasons, scored by a (checked) LLM judge.
Context relevance
Whether the retrieved chunks contain what the answer needs.
p50 / p95 latency
Typical and slow-case response time, ideally per span.

    Concept Debate

    Graded · part of 5% CD categoryDiscussion board · 3 stages
    This week's question

    Is an LLM-judge score a trustworthy production metric for an agent's reasoning accuracy?

    You'll argue the side your debate team is assigned, then argue the other one, then drop advocacy and synthesize.

    Your side this HOS

    Sides rotate each HOS. In HOS 3, Debate Team 1 argues Side A and Debate Team 2 argues Side B. Find your team under Communication → Groups in Brightspace. Not in a team? Before posting Stage 1, count the posts on each side and join the side with fewer; if they're tied, take Side A.

    Side A — Yes, with checksTeam 1 this HOS

    A calibrated judge, checked against human grading, is the only way to score every run.

    • Humans can't grade thousands of traces a day.
    • Agreement checks make the judge's error measurable.
    • A consistent, imperfect metric beats no metric.

    Side B — No, not on its ownTeam 2 this HOS

    Judge scores are opinions from another model; treat them as a hint.

    • Judges share blind spots with the model they grade.
    • A judge's leniency can drift when its own model version changes.
    • Business outcomes (resolved tickets, returns) are the real ground truth.
    Stage 1 · by Tue

    Initial position

    Argue your assigned side with one specific claim and one piece of evidence — a run, a trace, a simulator setting. Not a topic summary.
    Stage 2 · by Thu

    Argue the opposite

    Before replying to anyone, post the strongest version of the side you didn't hold.
    Stage 3 · by Sat

    Synthesis

    Drop advocacy. State a real position, citing at least one specific point from a classmate's post.
    Weak synthesis"LLM judges are useful but not perfect."
    Strong synthesis"Tomas pointed out that our judge's model version changed mid-term and scores jumped 6 points overnight with no agent change. So I'd trust the judge only as a trend line, pinned to a model version, re-checked against 20 human grades every time either version changes."

    Grading: Participation — initial position plus response to at least one peer (50%) · Writing — quality of argument and reasoning (50%).
