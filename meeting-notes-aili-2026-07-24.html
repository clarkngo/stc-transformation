Governance — Confirmed

Institute-Level Entity (ILE), reporting line, and STC ownership
Structure
The proposal is an Institute-Level Entity (ILE): it reports directly to the President's Office, holds its own budget, and shares a joint steering committee with STC and other university leadership. Other departments hold a liaison relationship to it rather than ownership — this is deliberate, to avoid turf conflicts and let the ILE move across schools quickly.
Not Chief of Staff
The President explicitly ruled out a Chief-of-Staff reporting line in this meeting — the line is the President's Office directly. This resolves the ambiguity flagged in the July 23 prep notes, where Chief of Staff was still floating as an open, unsettled option.
STC ownership retained
The President pushed back on treating this as a spin-off independent of STC: "if everyone owns it, no one owns it." STC keeps substantive ownership and staffing through this soft-launch phase; the President's-Office reporting line is about agility and signaling, not about STC losing the initiative.
Framing note
Avoid calling this a "task force" in any internal or board-facing material — the President wants it described as an internal STC project/small dedicated team, since university audiences over-read signals coming out of the President's Office.
🏷️

Naming & Branding — Open

"AI Lifelong Institute" (AILI) is too narrow; not yet finalized
Why it's changing
The President felt "AI" as the top-level name is too narrow — she wants room to cover cybersecurity, quantum, and future specialties without a rebrand each time, and named underserved recruiting targets (TikTok, Nintendo, Pinterest cybersecurity/tech roles in Seattle) the current AI-only framing doesn't obviously speak to.
Direction
Keep "Institute" as the umbrella; push AI down to be one specialty/track underneath it, alongside siblings like cybersecurity and quantum. Two umbrella candidates surfaced: "CityU X" / "CDUX" (echoing edX, 10X) and "Institute for Cyber & Emerging Technologies."
Domains
CDUX.edu as the official, President-approved domain, plus a separate internal/practice domain for testing. A CDUX.com was also floated specifically for the later paid marketplace phase — owning the marketplace relationship rather than renting it from a Coursera-style platform.
Not decided: the umbrella name is explicitly unresolved as of this meeting. Don't treat "CityU X" as final in board materials — confirm with Clark/Sam before it's used as the public-facing name.
🎓

Platform, IP & Revenue Model

Open edX, CC-BY licensing, and a tiered revenue plan
Platform
Self-hosted Open edX "core shell" (currently ~$78/mo on Sam's own hosting) — free/open-source, doesn't require a CityU email (lets outside audiences like the iKW Korean partnership in), and supports bring-your-own-course content.
Licensing
Open content uses Creative Commons Attribution (CC BY). Watch for third-party copyright issues when importing outside courses.
Short-term / internal
  • Service-based support
  • Data sharing with marketing
  • Instructor training/enablement
Mid-term
  • Continuing-ed & industry certification workshops
  • Contract/compliance training (e.g. a police department pays for a custom course; CityU also trains their staff to co-teach it)
  • Internal course licensing between CityU schools
Long-term / external (B2C & B2B)
  • E-commerce marketplace, B2C first then B2B
  • Digital badges
  • External organizational partnerships

Funnel & Ebooks — Validated

TOFU → MOFU → BOFU → Conversion, confirmed as-is
President's reaction: "really smart," "fantastic" — the TOFU→MOFU→BOFU→Conversion funnel and the six-touchpoint model (YouTube, GitHub, Open edX, Workshop, Seminar, Community Hub) were validated without changes. Full architecture lives in the CityUX Open Ecosystem Execution Plan.
Gated ebooks confirmed as TOFU
Branded "CityU Guides" ebooks (e.g. "AI Skills Every IT Professional Needs by 2027," "Anxiety to Confidence") capture name/email/persona into a marketing Google Sheet alongside YouTube as a top-of-funnel node.
Persona options trimmed
Lead-capture persona choices are: prospective student, current student, corporate/employer, educational community. The standalone "partner" option was cut on the President's instruction as premature.
🎯

Target Personas — Still Too Broad

Open action item, not finalized
The President rejected "everyone" as a target and asked for 2-3 concrete personas before the next round of materials. The candidates below were raised in discussion but not narrowed down or prioritized.
AI-anxious degree-seekers
Feel pressured into an AI-adjacent credential without being technical themselves.
Self-taught, uncredentialed
Helpdesk workers, gamers, hobbyists who are informally skilled but have no formal credential to show for it.
"Second-chance" adult learners
Career-interrupted or career-changing nontraditional students.
Budget-constrained nonprofits
YMCA-style organizations with small but real professional-development budgets.
