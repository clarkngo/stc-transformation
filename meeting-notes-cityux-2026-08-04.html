Umbrella Naming — Still Open

"CityUx" floated as a working name; President has not decided
Why a high-level name matters
The team revisited whether the initiative needs a top-level name at all — concluding it does, and that it should convey ownership rather than being purely descriptive. This continues the naming thread from the July 24 meeting, where "CityU X" / "CDUX" and "Institute for Cyber & Emerging Technologies" were the two candidates on the table.
"CityUx" as a candidate
"CityUx" was discussed as working well specifically because it's broad and generic — the President wants a name broad enough to cover future specialties (echoes the July 24 direction of keeping AI as one specialty under a wider umbrella, not the umbrella itself).
Not decided: the President has not confirmed "CityUx" or any other option. Plan is to present naming options at the next meeting and check her reaction.
❓

Interface Ownership — Unclear

Flagged as ambiguous in the source notes — needs confirmation
The notes reference deciding on a name for "the interface" and mention "SPD" as a possible reference point, with the President asking which department or person would be responsible for it. This is ambiguous in the raw transcript — it's unclear whether "SPD" refers to the Seattle PD partnership already in motion, a different acronym, or a mis-transcription. Recommend clarifying with the group before this goes into any external-facing material.
Naming bar for the interface
Whatever name is chosen, it should be generic yet specific enough to convey knowledge — for the audience of people trying to reskill, not just a vague label.
🎯

Target Audience & Partner Groups

Community/club targets macro-level credential seekers outside CityU
Audience
The community/club is aimed at students and professionals outside CityU who want a macro-level credential. Team flagged the need for a concrete example/use case that resonates with this audience — not yet defined.
Two feeder groups
STC (Students in the City) and CityU (formerly CTU) — both groups can attend sessions. An existing partnership lets the team invite people into Open edX to host both CityU's own content and partner content, consistent with the platform direction from July 24.
🚀

First Project Scope

Self-contained, no external help needed
Scope
The first project is meant to be self-contained — buildable without external help. It targets technical people who want to develop their skills; many of them come in with only a computer-science background.
Deliverable
A single-principle, multi-agent framework — the concrete artifact this first cohort/project builds toward.
📚

Curriculum & Pedagogy

Three tracks, flipped-classroom delivery
Web Design
5 major modules
Coding
5 major modules
AI Programming / AI Engineering
Track structure not yet detailed
Delivery model
"Learning by doing" first, then "learning by lecture" — a flipped classroom applying Bloom's Taxonomy. Each module uses pre-testing and post-testing, and opens with a "rejoice" concept that serves as the module's guiding philosophy.
🌏

IKW Korea Demo — Proof Point

Strong reception, useful evidence for the President pitch
The IKW summer camp in Korea gave a 30-minute demo that drew roughly 180 attendees, including Korean students, with high participant satisfaction. This is the same iKW Korean-student partnership referenced in the July 24 platform notes as a reason Open edX (no CityU email required) matters. Worth citing as evidence when presenting audience demand.
🖥️

Website Build

Community playground, five modules across three tracks
Purpose
A website covering the web design, coding, and AI modules above, positioned as a playground for the community — a place to showcase projects and host content, not just deliver courses.
