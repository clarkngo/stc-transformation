Track 1: Habitat Technology & Organizational Transformation

Habitat for Humanity Seattle-King & Kittitas Counties

Strategic context and long-term vision

  • National and local structure: Habitat runs through a national organization and local affiliates, for example King, Pierce, and Snohomish counties.
  • 3–5 year horizon: under CEO Brett Dantano and CFO Scott Slater, technology modernization is a designated 3–5 year strategic initiative.
  • A product other affiliates can use: clean up internal data, metrics, and automation (including the 99-year land trust and custom financial and banking workflows). Then package it as a standard, scalable technology offering that other affiliates can subscribe to or adopt.

Order of work (strict)

  1. IT infrastructure audit: cloud vs. on-premise servers, password and security management, and governance policies.
  2. Requirements and inventory: a standard framework that makes it easy for affiliates to enter data and take stock of their systems.
  3. Data infrastructure and management: consistent, reliable data to support financial metrics and reporting.
  4. Process improvement: remove duplicated work and manual bottlenecks.
  5. Automation and agentic AI: added only after the data and process groundwork is solid.

People and change management

  • The human side is the hard part: research on ERP rollouts shows people and culture block scaling more than software does.
  • Knowledge repository: write down knowledge that currently lives only in staff members' heads, find the gaps, and reduce the risk when staff leave.
  • Leadership and talent: Jim acts as strategic champion from the board and advisory side. He will bring in specialized technical talent, contracted either directly by Habitat or through Jim's LLC.
How this changes the pre-meeting plan: the prep page put Habitat work in "Phase 2, after data approval." The meeting goes further: at Habitat, AI is step 5 of 5. Early value comes from the audit, requirements, and data work.

Track 2: AI Fluency & Course Design (CityU EMBA)

School of Business & Management

EMBA course development

  • Baldridge Framework: the EMBA program is aligned to the Baldridge Framework.
  • EMV 582 (Product/Service Innovation): the first-quarter course is written and is moving to instructional design.
  • AI integration over the next 6–9 months:
    • Bucket A, course operations: use agentic workflows for LMS integrations, routine grading help, and communication between students and the instructor.
    • Bucket B, AI fluency and module challenges: hands-on AI challenges in every module, such as cash-flow analysis, break-even calculations, competitive analysis, and research agents.
  • Project LEO (3–5 year horizon): a space-economy project led by Dr. Pors with Amazon. It would deliver CityU courses and certificates to underserved regions over satellite infrastructure.

Teaching approach: flipping Bloom's Taxonomy

  • Practice first: start with hands-on scenarios and introduce theory afterward.
  • AI as a Socratic tutor: use AI to prepare students before class and as an adaptive tutor, not just as a text generator.
  • Scenario-based knowledge checks: replace rote multiple-choice tests with ungraded scenario checks, spaced out over time, so students retain more.
STC overlap: the practice-first, AI-as-tutor approach matches STC's teaching framework and the hands-on design of AI 420. That makes SBM × STC course-design collaboration a natural next step.
