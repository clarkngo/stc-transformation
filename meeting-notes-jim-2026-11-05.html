Research Charter and Drafting Plan V2

Revised draft dated Nov 5, for joint approval. Bracketed items in the charter are still to be confirmed.

Working title

Judgment in Human–AI Work: A Sociomaterial Approach to Authentic Assessment with CEAU. The alternative keeps the Bloom framing: Rethinking Bloom's Pyramid for Human–AI Work. Either replaces the Oct 30 title, "Beyond Bloom's Pyramid."

Stance

  • Bloom is a reference point, not a target. The paper argues that AI spreads the thinking Bloom describes across people and tools, so assessment should follow that shift. It doesn't claim to disprove the taxonomy.
  • Sociomateriality: agency is relational. It comes from how people, tools, and tasks are entangled. The anchoring strand (for example, Orlikowski) is still to be named.
  • Agentic AI gets a boundary section only, not a separate framework. Generative AI anchors the design case.

Research questions

  • How does AI change the entanglement of human and material agency across creating, evaluating, analyzing, and understanding?
  • How can CEAU turn sociomaterial principles into assessment of judgment, understanding, and demonstrable competence?

Method and evidence

  • Phase 1 (this paper): a selective, theory-building literature review plus instructional design artifacts. No claims about learning effectiveness.
  • Guarding against self-confirmation: CEAU is Clark's own design, so the paper discloses that, sets fit criteria in advance, adds at least one contrasting external case, and states where the framework strains.
  • Outputs: a framework diagram, assessment principles, an EMBA transfer example, and testable propositions, each tagged with the evidence a Phase 2 study would need.

Publication route

Two routes: the ACBSP forum and a CityU-supported journal. The primary route was to be chosen at the Nov 13 meeting, once the forum's call details were in hand. The other becomes a follow-on. ACBSP's site confirms the dates, place, and theme; Jan 19 is a planning target, not a verified deadline.

What Joel's note says about IRB timing

Useful for planning Phase 2. Student dissertations go through an expedited review:

  • After a cursory check, the committee reviews in detail. The chair usually hears back from reviewers in 1–2 weeks.
  • The result is either approval or a request for more information. A request is the most common outcome, usually to clarify procedures such as participant selection or consent.
  • Fixes are often an email reply or an edited consent form, usually done within a week. Approval generally follows.
  • Joel is working on making the IRB process easier to understand and welcomes questions.

The faculty-research route for Phase 2 may differ from the expedited student-dissertation route. Ask Joel which applies.
