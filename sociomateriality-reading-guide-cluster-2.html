The cluster in 60 seconds

Cluster 1 said agency comes from how people and things are put together. These papers ask what that means once AI is one of the things, and sometimes acts on its own. The shared answer: AI doesn't “have” agency or “lack” it. What it does depends on where and how it's set up in the work, and the same system can play different roles from one moment to the next.

💻 From your field

Think of a bot added to your team's Slack and repo. Whether it helps, gets ignored, or causes chaos isn't a property of the model. It depends on its permissions, where it posts, who reviews its PRs, and the team's unwritten rules about pinging people.

🍎 From your field

Think of an AI tutor added to a course. Whether it supports learning, becomes an answer machine, or sits unused isn't decided by the model. It depends on where it appears, what the assignment rewards, and what the teacher expects students to do with it.

📈 From your field

Think of AI agents added to a strategy or HR process. Whether they open the process up or quietly take decisions away from people depends on how they're configured, who reviews their output, and what the organization rewards, not on the vendor's feature list.

Paper 1 of 5

Exploring AI-in-the-making: Sociomaterial Genealogies of AI Performativity

Scott, S. V., & Orlikowski, W. J. (2025). Information and Organization, 35, 100558. doi:10.1016/j.infoandorg.2025.100558 (open access)

Core: the 2025 update of the theoryConceptualAgential realism

In one sentence

AI is not a finished “thing” but a set of practices always in the making, so researchers should trace how different versions of AI get enacted, and with what consequences.

The argument

The authors argue that “AI” lumps together very different techniques and treats them as one stable object. Borrowing Callon's idea of markets “in the making,” they treat AI as AI-in-the-making: always being reconfigured through practice. Drawing on Barad's agential realism, they say agency in the age of AI is no different from before: it has always been the ongoing reconfiguring of the world. What's new is the conditions AI creates and how many versions of AI get enacted.

Their method proposal is a sociomaterial genealogy: trace how a given version of AI came to be enacted in practice, what it made possible, and who is accountable for its effects.

Key terms

AI-in-the-making
AI as ongoing practice and reconfiguration, never a finished, uniform object.
Performativity
What AI is shows up in what it does in practice; its effects make it what it is.
Sociomaterial genealogy
A way of studying how particular versions of AI came about and what consequences they produce.

From your field

💻 Software engineer

"The model" is never one thing. The production system is a specific model version, a system prompt, retrieval sources, guardrails, and an eval suite, all changed weekly. A sociomaterial genealogy is like reading the git history and the incident log together: how did this version of the assistant come to behave the way it does, and who changed what?

🍎 Teacher

"ChatGPT in my class" isn't one thing either. A student on a free tier, a district-approved tutor with guardrails, and a model inside the LMS are different versions of AI, enacted differently. The genealogy question for a teacher: how did the AI my students actually use come to be configured this way, and who decided?

📈 Business

"We're deploying AI" hides many versions: the pilot, the vendor's update, the compliance team's restrictions, the shadow use on personal phones. The authors' point for managers: accountability comes from tracing which version produced which outcome, not from a single "AI policy."

Why it matters for our paper

This is the most direct theoretical anchor for "effective AI": it lets us say effective AI is a version of AI enacted in a particular learning setting, not a property of a model. Pairs naturally with Orlikowski 2007 in the theory section.

Check yourself

1. What does “AI-in-the-making” push against?

Show answer

Treating AI as one finished, uniform, stable thing. The authors see many versions, always being reconfigured through practice.

2. According to the authors, is agency different in the age of AI?

Show answer

No. Agency has always been the ongoing reconfiguring of the world. What's different is the conditions AI creates and the many versions of AI being enacted.

Paper 2 of 5

Exploring How Knowledge Workers Situate an Organisationally Embedded LLM Agent in their Collaborative Work Practices

O'Hara, K., Massimi, M., & Khan, M. (2026). Computer Supported Cooperative Work, 35, 16. doi:10.1007/s10606-026-09549-1

Core: empirical, enterprise LLMQualitative, 14 participantsIT services firm

In one sentence

When an LLM agent is built into a company's systems, it doesn't just speed up individual tasks; it reorganizes how meetings, documents, and collaboration fit together.

The argument

Most LLM studies look at individuals using off-the-shelf chatbots. This one studies an IT services firm that built its own LLM agent into its enterprise systems, documents, and workflows, through interviews and observation with 14 employees over time.

Findings: meetings became persistent, searchable artifacts; work products emerged from long chains of human–AI interaction instead of single authors; and people changed how they talked, adding context and structuring speech so the agent's transcripts and summaries would be useful later. The authors conclude that such agents work as organizational infrastructure, not standalone productivity tools, which brings new questions of accountability and risk.

Key terms

Situated practice
Looking at what people actually do with a tool in context, over time.
LLM as infrastructure
The agent becomes part of the background systems work runs on, like email or a document store.

From your field

💻 Software engineer

It's like adding a bot that records stand-ups and indexes them. Soon people start saying ticket numbers out loud and spelling out decisions so the bot will capture them. The tool changed the meeting, not just the notes. That's the paper's core finding.

🍎 Teacher

Picture class sessions that are automatically transcribed and searchable. Students start asking questions “for the record,” and group work starts to happen through the transcript as much as in the room. The tool reshapes participation, not just note-taking.

📈 Business

Enterprise AI's value isn't in minutes saved per task. It's in how knowledge flows: meetings become reusable records, handoffs change, and new risks appear around who's accountable for AI-assembled documents. Measure the reorganization, not just the speed.

Why it matters for our paper

Strong evidence that people adapt their own behavior to the AI, a clear sociomaterial point. For CEAU: students will also adapt how they write and think to fit the tool, which is why assessment should look at the process.

Check yourself

1. How did workers change their own behavior because of the agent?

Show answer

They structured their talk and added context so the agent's transcription, summaries, and retrieval would be more useful.

2. What's the paper's main reframing of LLMs?

Show answer

From standalone productivity tools to organizational infrastructure that reorganizes collaboration.

Paper 3 of 5

Open Strategy and AI: A New Actor in the Strategy Process?

Hautz, J., Stadler, C., & Whittington, R. (2026). Long Range Planning, 59, 102693. doi:10.1016/j.lrp.2026.102693 (open access)

Supporting: a frameworkShort perspective piece4As framework

In one sentence

Generative AI is joining strategy-making as a possible new actor, and it can open the process up or close it down, so research needs a differentiated framework.

The argument

The paper opens with a consulting firm that went from 30 consultants to three by running client strategy processes with a network of more than 35 AI agents. The authors propose a 4As framework for AI's role in open strategy: affordances (what AI makes possible), agency (AI acting on its own account), automation (AI replacing human tasks), and augmentation (AI extending human roles).

Drawing on sociomateriality and Actor-Network Theory, they argue AI's effects will be ambiguous: more transparency and inclusion in some ways, and selectivity, black-boxing, deskilling, and substitution in others.

Key terms

Open strategy
Making strategy more transparent and including people beyond top management.
4As
Affordances, agency, automation, augmentation: four lenses on AI's role.

From your field

💻 Software engineer

The 4As map onto a familiar product debate. Affordance: what the API lets you do. Automation: the cron job that replaces a person. Augmentation: the copilot that makes a person faster. Agency: the autonomous agent that decides and acts. Same model, four very different roles.

🍎 Teacher

Use the 4As to sort AI in a course. Automation: auto-graded quizzes. Augmentation: students drafting with AI, then revising. Affordance: what the tool makes possible, like instant feedback at 2 a.m. Agency: an AI that decides what a student studies next.

📈 Business

A ready-made checklist for any AI rollout: are we automating, augmenting, giving people new affordances, or handing agency to the system? Each one has different risks for openness, skills, and accountability.

Why it matters for our paper

The 4As offer a quick way to classify AI's role in a CEAU activity. Useful as a supporting citation; the case of three consultants replacing 30 is a vivid opener.

Check yourself

1. Name the 4As.

Show answer

Affordances, agency, automation, augmentation.

2. Does the paper predict AI will make strategy more open?

Show answer

Not simply. It argues effects will be ambiguous: more openness in some ways, more closure (selectivity, black-boxing, deskilling) in others.

Paper 4 of 5

Recurrent AI Assemblage Forms in Radiological Interpretation Routines

Berrahou, M. (2026). Proceedings of the 47th International Conference on Information Systems (ICIS 2026), Lisbon. Completed research paper.

Core: the clearest empirical caseQualitative, cancer centerOrganizational routines

In one sentence

The same AI tool for spotting lung nodules is used in three different ways within one radiology routine, depending on how the case, the timing, and the systems line up.

The argument

Studies usually treat AI's place in a workflow as fixed: it's either the “second reader” or the “first reader.” This qualitative study of an AI pulmonary-nodule detection tool in a cancer center finds the same artifact recurrently assembled in three forms:

  • Sustained: a second-look verification device.
  • Desynchronized: bypassed when material or clinical misalignments make the output unusable.
  • Aligned: a first-look triage filter, when it's available early enough.

Radiologists navigate between these forms exam by exam. AI's role can't be read off its accuracy, user trust, or the workflow mode on paper.

Key terms

Assemblage form
A recurring configuration of people, tool, case, and timing in which the AI plays a particular role.
Organizational routine
A repeated, recognizable pattern of work, here reading a scan.

From your field

💻 Software engineer

Like a static-analysis tool in CI. Sometimes it's the second check after review (sustained). Sometimes it times out or flags the wrong branch, so everyone ignores it (desynchronized). Sometimes it runs first and decides what humans look at (aligned). Same tool, three roles, depending on the pipeline that day.

🍎 Teacher

Like a plagiarism or AI detector. Sometimes it's a second check after the teacher reads (sustained). Sometimes the report arrives too late or doesn't match the assignment type, so it's skipped (desynchronized). Sometimes it runs first and decides which essays get a closer look (aligned).

📈 Business

Like a fraud-scoring model. Some days it backs up analysts' judgment, some days data gaps make it useless, and some days it triages the queue before anyone looks. A dashboard showing “AI adoption: yes” hides all three.

Why it matters for our paper

An excellent empirical illustration of Orlikowski's technologies-in-practice, updated for AI: one artifact, several enacted forms. Supports measuring how learners enact AI, not just whether they used it.

Check yourself

1. Name the three assemblage forms.

Show answer

Sustained (second-look verification), desynchronized (bypassed due to misalignments), aligned (first-look triage).

2. Why can't AI's role be read off its accuracy?

Show answer

Because whether the output is available, interpretable, and actionable depends on timing, systems, the clinical case, and professional norms in each exam.

Paper 5 of 5

Working with Agentic 'Teammates': When a New Organizational Actor Collides with the Human Ecosystem of Work

Qadri, R., Denton, R., Madaio, M., et al. (2026). arXiv preprint arXiv:2609.29901 [cs.HC].

Core: agentic AI in the wildQualitative, in situPreprint, not yet peer reviewed

In one sentence

When a proactive AI agent “teammate” is deployed across real teams, it triggers breakdowns and negotiations over unwritten rules, boundaries, trust, and who's really in charge.

The argument

Most research on AI teammates uses lab tasks. This in-situ qualitative study looks at a persistent, proactive agent deployed across multiple teams in a large technology company. It sits in team chats and documents, monitors context, and runs multi-step work in the background.

The boundaries of the human–agent workplace turned out to be in flux, with breakdowns and negotiations across three areas: (1) the tacit rules of collaborative workflows; (2) the relational boundaries of this new non-human actor; and (3) the redistribution of trust and human agency. The authors call for deliberate institutional scaffolding and design that preserves human agency.

Key terms

Proactive agent
An AI that acts without being asked: it monitors, decides, and executes.
Tacit rules
Unwritten norms of teamwork, like when it's OK to interrupt or who answers what.

From your field

💻 Software engineer

Imagine a bot that opens PRs, comments in threads, and pings people on its own. Suddenly the team has to decide things nobody ever wrote down: can it @-mention the lead? Should it reply in the incident channel? Who owns its mistakes? Those negotiations are the findings.

🍎 Teacher

Imagine an AI teaching assistant that posts in the class forum on its own, nudges students who haven't submitted, and answers questions at midnight. Students and teachers must work out new rules: when it should stay quiet, who is responsible when it's wrong, and whether students trust it over peers.

📈 Business

Agentic AI isn't an IT rollout; it's adding an organizational actor. You need the equivalent of an onboarding: role, permissions, escalation paths, and who's accountable. Without that, the breakdowns land on the people around it.

Why it matters for our paper

Strong support for the boundary section on agentic AI. It shows agentic AI disrupts tacit norms, an argument for keeping humans as visible, accountable participants in any AI-enabled learning design.

Check yourself

1. Name the three areas where breakdowns and negotiations happened.

Show answer

Tacit rules of collaborative workflows; the agent's relational boundaries; the redistribution of trust and human agency.

2. Why does the study's setting matter?

Show answer

It's a real, in-situ deployment over time, not a lab task, so it captures the messy, tacit realities of collaboration.
