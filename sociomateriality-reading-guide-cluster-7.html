The cluster in 60 seconds

Most studies of technology take a snapshot. These papers insist that change happens over time, often slowly, often out of sync, and often below the surface. Digital transformation can be "always tomorrow"; routines can lull organizations into missing problems; and the deepest effects can be an undertow you don't see until it has moved you.

💻 From your field

Think of a migration that's been “next quarter” for three years, or a dependency upgrade blocked by another team's release cycle. Systems change at different speeds, and the gaps between those speeds are where projects stall.

🍎 From your field

Curriculum change moves at the speed of accreditation, while AI tools change monthly and students change every term. These papers explain why “we'll update the course next year” keeps happening, and why some effects only show up years later.

📈 From your field

Strategy decks show transformation as a timeline. Reality is layers moving at different speeds (technology, teams, infrastructure, regulation), plus slow institutional shifts nobody planned. These papers give you language for that.

Paper 1 of 4

The Digital Undertow and Institutional Displacement: A Sociomaterial Approach

Orlikowski, W. J., & Scott, S. V. (2023). Organization Theory, 4, 1–16. doi:10.1177/26317877231180898 (open access)

Core: strong sociomateriality, recentConceptual with two sectorsHospitality and finance

In one sentence

Beneath the visible waves of digital transformation, an undertow is quietly displacing the institutions (rating systems, regulators, standards) that used to hold authority in an industry.

The argument

Digital transformation is usually studied through its visible, dramatic changes. Orlikowski and Scott argue there's also a digital undertow: less visible, indirect effects that displace institutional values, norms, and rules. Examples include hospitality, where online review platforms challenge official star-rating schemes, and finance, where cryptocurrencies don't fit the apparatus built to regulate currencies.

They argue conventional tools aren't built to see these corollary effects, and that strong sociomateriality, a relational and performative approach, is well suited to studying them.

Key terms

Digital undertow
Indirect, less visible effects of digitalization that reshape institutions over time.
Institutional displacement
When established bodies and rules lose their primacy and authority.

From your field

💻 Software engineer

The wave is the new framework everyone migrates to. The undertow is how GitHub stars and Stack Overflow answers quietly replaced official docs and certifications as the authority on what's “good,” without anyone deciding that.

🍎 Teacher

The wave is AI in the classroom. The undertow may be the quiet displacement of institutional authorities like grades, transcripts, and accreditation as signals of what someone can do. Those effects come slowly and from the side.

📈 Business

Watch the undertow, not just the wave. Your industry's gatekeepers (rating agencies, certifiers, regulators) may be losing authority to platforms without any headline event. Strategy should scan for those displacements.

Why it matters for our paper

Orlikowski's own recent statement of “strong sociomateriality,” useful to show the lens is current. The undertow idea also frames longer-run effects of AI on credentials, relevant to stackable certificates.

Paper 2 of 4

Heavy Time: Sensemaking and the Temporal Experience

Patriotta, G., & Weick, K. E. (2026). Journal of Management Studies, 63(8). doi:10.1111/joms.70163 (open access)

Supporting: why breakdowns teachConceptual essayNewsrooms, manufacturing

In one sentence

Sensemaking theory has always contained a theory of time: in routine “light time” organizations stop noticing, and in breakdowns “heavy time” makes them notice and learn.

The argument

Weick once admitted that his work rarely addressed time. This essay, completed after his death, argues that sensemaking was always a theory of how people and organizations inhabit time. Drawing on phenomenology and Thomas Mann, it contrasts light time, the fluent flow of routine that frees attention but dulls perception, with heavy time, the thick, consequential time of breakdowns that forces reconstruction and learning.

Using newsrooms and manufacturing, it shows that routine curbs learning and hides vulnerability, while incidents restore the capacity to notice. Organizations live forward but understand themselves backward.

Key terms

Light time
Routine time that passes unnoticed; efficient but dulling.
Heavy time
Time thickened by a breakdown, when details become visible and learning happens.

From your field

💻 Software engineer

Steady-state on-call is light time: nobody reads the logs. An outage is heavy time: suddenly everyone sees every detail, and the postmortem is where learning happens. Routine recovery without a postmortem throws that learning away.

🍎 Teacher

When AI makes every assignment smooth, learning risks becoming light time. Productive struggle (a bug, a contradiction, a failed draft) is heavy time. Designing small, safe breakdowns into a course may be what makes learning stick.

📈 Business

Smooth operations hide vulnerability. Build mechanisms that register small deviations, and treat incidents as learning episodes rather than things to resolve and forget.

Why it matters for our paper

Supporting. Gives a theoretical reason why frictionless AI help can undercut learning, and why CEAU's refine and revise loops (small breakdowns) matter.

Check yourself

1. Contrast light and heavy time.

Show answer

Light time is routine and fluent but dulls perception; heavy time comes with breakdowns and makes noticing and learning possible.

2. Why does routine recovery curb learning?

Show answer

It returns the organization to light time quickly, without the reconstruction that turns an incident into learning.

Paper 3 of 4

Always Tomorrow: The Making of the Filled Present in Digitalization

Tinjan, M. (2026). Doctoral dissertation, University of Gothenburg. Gothenburg Studies in Informatics, report 77. hdl.handle.net/2077/93142

Supporting: why change stallsDissertation, five studiesSwedish public sector

In one sentence

Public-sector digitalization keeps going even when the promised transformation never arrives, because projected futures pile up in the present and make present action wait on something else.

The argument

Drawing on five studies of Swedish public-sector settings, the dissertation reframes digitalization as a temporal organizing phenomenon, following what gets done and what gets left undone. Governance models, strategies, and architectures came with waiting, pauses, non-scaling, and non-decommissioning, justified by talk of resources, law, and risk.

Its theory of (un)becoming digitalization has two mechanisms: reoriented agency (present action depends on something unresolved, elsewhere, or later) and future saturation (projected futures accumulate in the present). Together they produce a filled present: the work of digitalization continues while the digitalization of work stays "always tomorrow."

Key terms

Filled present
A present so crowded with projected futures and pending dependencies that action keeps being deferred.
Reoriented agency
Present action made contingent on matters elsewhere or later.

From your field

💻 Software engineer

"We'll retire the legacy system after the new platform launches, which is after the data migration, which is after the vendor contract..." That's a filled present. Projects stay busy and nothing is decommissioned.

🍎 Teacher

"We'll redesign assessment for AI once the new LMS is in, once the policy is final, once accreditation is done." Course change stays perpetually next term. Naming the filled present helps break it: pick one change you can make now.

📈 Business

Transformation programs can be full of activity and still not transform anything. Watch for non-decommissioning and repeated “next phase” talk; they're symptoms of a filled present, not of slow progress.

Why it matters for our paper

Supporting, mainly for the project itself: it warns against an AI-in-education agenda that is always “after the pilot.” It's a dissertation, so cite the specific published papers inside it where possible.

Check yourself

1. What is the “filled present”?

Show answer

A present crowded with projected futures and pending conditions that keep deferring action.

2. Name the two mechanisms.

Show answer

Reoriented agency and future saturation.

Paper 4 of 4

Layered Temporality in Digital Service Innovation

Elo, J., Jalo, H., Torro, O., & Pirkkalainen, H. (2026). ICIS 2026 Proceedings. Short paper. AIS eLibrary

Peripheral: layers moving at different speedsShort paper, study in progressFinnish construction

In one sentence

Digital, organizational, infrastructural, and institutional layers change at different speeds, and those differences matter only when a new digital combination depends on a layer that's out of step.

The argument

The paper introduces layered temporality: differences in pace across interdependent digital, organizational, infrastructural, and institutional layers. Differences aren't a problem in themselves. They become temporal misalignment when a digital innovation depends on a layer that's out of step. Actors respond with temporal work: not necessarily syncing layers, but resequencing, adjusting expectations, reshaping dependencies, or living with the gap.

It's a short paper outlining an ongoing study in the Finnish construction sector.

Key terms

Temporal misalignment
A pace difference between layers that blocks something that depends on both.
Temporal work
Actions people take to cope with or reshape pace differences.

From your field

💻 Software engineer

Your service is ready; the shared data standard isn't; the regulator's rule changes next year. Temporal work is feature-flagging, adapters, and resequencing the roadmap rather than waiting for everything to sync.

🍎 Teacher

AI tools (fast), course design (medium), and accreditation and policy (slow) move at different speeds. You don't need them aligned, just a workable arrangement where your course depends on them.

📈 Business

When an innovation stalls, ask which layer it's waiting on and whether you can reshape the dependency, rather than assuming technology is simply “ahead” of the organization.

Why it matters for our paper

Peripheral. Possibly useful for Paper 2's feasibility discussion (device, connectivity, language, and institutional layers moving at different speeds).

Check yourself

1. When does a temporal difference become misalignment?

Show answer

When a cross-layer dependency makes it matter for a digital innovation.

2. Does temporal work always mean synchronizing layers?

Show answer

No. It can mean resequencing, adjusting expectations, reshaping dependencies, or accommodating continued differences.
